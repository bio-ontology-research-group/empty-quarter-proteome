Root judgement, 15 October 2026

F3 merits a bounded main-text case study. It asks how the genome resource extends sequence coverage within a recognizable protein family. The candidates were analysed independently of the UniRef50-dark classification: recognizing a profile does not by itself establish whether a sequence passes that separate UniRef50 search rule. We therefore do not assert that the two cohorts are disjoint without an explicit join.

All 45 reference-site-conserving EQ heliorhodopsins have qualifying homologues in the expanded published panel. Their median best-bit-score hit identity is 57.3%; the 24 Patescibacteriota loci have median 39.4%. These are divergent homologues, not evidence of a new family, new fold or demonstrated light-response mechanism. The original 25-record reference panel was insufficient to support the case alone, so we added the 1,566 qualified sequences from the published Bulzu et al. 2021 alignment.

Within Saccharimonadia, equal ten-sequence draws still add about 7.3 clusters absent from this reference after trimming terminal extensions or restricting EQ to loci passing the read gate. This is additional coverage of an explicit panel. GEM partly overlaps that panel, and its zero added clusters cannot mean zero diversity. The reference-free sensitivity gives 8.04 versus four core clusters at 50% identity and 9.605 versus ten at 80%, explicitly demonstrating resolution dependence. The sampled source panels and taxonomy versions remain limitations.

Full-length translations match all 45 EQ source contigs. Original quality-filtered assembly mappings support 43/45 under the stated depth and breadth criteria; two weaker loci remain flagged. All 45 ESMFold models pass mean CA pLDDT 70 and query-normalized TM 0.5 to 6SU3. These predictions support compatibility with a known fold and share sequence-derived evidence with other tests. Read coverage is useful independent evidence of the assembled loci, but neither it nor predicted structures establishes activity or excludes every assembly error.

F1 belongs in supplementary ecological context. The strict CPF panel contains 914 complete genes in 775 MAGs. On 37 sites and 69 paired visits, the median surface excess is 8.47 percentage points (Holm p=0.00131). The exact mean decomposition is a different estimand: total 8.71 points, with 5.31 between shared genus groups, 2.38 from groups observed in only one position and 1.03 within shared groups. Unclassified genera are separate MAG groups and comprise 25.7% of mean abundance, limiting genus-resolved interpretation. This reuses the original data and mostly overlapping K01669 carriers, so it is not independent replication. Fixed traits cannot measure within-strain adaptation.

F4 is a supporting architecture and neighbourhood resource. Of 940 complete EctC predictions, 822 retain WEYH and 616 have qualifying complete A/B neighbours in 608 MAGs. Argon identified seven cases where A and B assignments are on the same ORF; the manuscript distinguishes these from the 609 loci with separate A and B ORFs. Independent inspection confirms non-overlapping A/B domain matches in all seven EQ cases; the fusion assignments remain predictions. Five predicted EctCD fusion ORFs have read coverage and 4–19 templates spanning the domain junction; one has FEYH and is outside the strict residue subset. The median ecological contrast is +0.56 points, raw p=0.0974 and Holm p=0.389. It does not establish surface enrichment. A positive descriptive mean/bootstrap result cannot substitute for that test.

F5 stays archived. It has broad environmental homology and supplies no added comparative question that warrants expanding this paper.

Root and Argon agree on the placement: F3 in a concise main-text subsection and figure; F1/F4 in the supplement with complete Methods. I do not adopt Argon's stronger statements that profile recognition proves exclusion from the UniRef50-dark cohort, that core trimming proves a particular functional mechanism, or that original mappings exclude chimeric artifacts. These follow-ups strengthen biological specificity and candidate credibility. They do not alone establish a mechanistic advance for Nature Communications or resolve the existing deposition and catalogue-wide reproducibility prerequisites.
