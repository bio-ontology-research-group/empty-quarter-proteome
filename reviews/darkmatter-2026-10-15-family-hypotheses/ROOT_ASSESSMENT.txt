ADDITIONAL BIOLOGICAL-DISCOVERY TRACK — HYPOTHESES FOR AUTHOR CHOICE
2026-10-15

Scope and status
This is an addition to H1-H4. Existing analyses, manuscript, and claim registries
are unchanged. Literature retrieval and inspection of existing small evidence
files only; no new family searches, embeddings, phylogenies, ecological tests,
remote jobs or experiments have been run. All F1-F5 below remain untested.
Gemini 3.8 Flash High contributed through agy; root checked and revised its
suggestions. The original report is retained with corrections in ROOT_AUDIT.txt.

Recommendation
Prioritise F1 (photolyase/cryptochrome diversification) and F2 (a specific dark
compatible-solute phosphatase family). Keep F3 (rhodopsins) as a bounded third
screen and F4/F5 as alternatives. A family should earn its place through a
coherent biological finding, corroborated family membership, and quantified
reference expansion. The broad candidate screen should be reported even if a
single family becomes the eventual paper example.

F1. EQ expands photolyase/cryptochrome diversity with reproducible light-niche
partitioning among particular subfamilies.

Hypothesis: the catalogue contains underrepresented photolyase/cryptochrome
lineages or domain architectures whose carriers are associated with surface
soil after accounting for host lineage, site and expedition. Sequence diversity
and ecological association are distinct endpoints; either may succeed alone.

Precedent: Singh et al. 2009 (10.1128/JB.01084-08) combined genomes and several
metagenomes to identify additional photolyase/cryptochrome groups. Xu et al.
2021 (10.1093/molbev/msab191) identified short photolyase-like proteins and
validated repair activity for a representative. These must be included among
the established reference classes; short/domain-reduced proteins are not
automatically truncated artifacts or novel classes.

Existing foothold: manuscript/main.tex reports 24.3% versus 12.1% mapped-community
photolyase carriage across 44 paired surface/subsurface sites. This motivates
a family-resolved test; it is not proof that unknown photolyases are present.
The within-species comparison is inconclusive, so species turnover remains an
important alternative. code/uv_survival_analysis.py and results/uv_survival/
contain older support, but the current manuscript/current-catalogue provenance
takes precedence over historical all-MAG counts.

Primary tests: quantify added nonredundant phylogenetic breadth and full-space
reference distance for each curated subfamily, then estimate site-paired
surface/subsurface carrier or gene-abundance contrasts with lineage adjustment.
Compare against a taxonomy-balanced reference set and matched additions from
external soils. Treat repeated samples as nested within sites/expeditions.

Evidence required: complete and read-supported loci, family profiles and
domain-aware alignments, supported phylogenetic placement, plausible cofactor/
substrate-binding architecture, consistent context across independent genomes.
Discriminate repair-capable photolyases from related signalling cryptochromes.
An enzyme assay would establish repair activity and substrate class; current
DNA evidence supports genetic potential and occurrence.

Downgrade/stop: candidates lie entirely inside already sampled reference
diversity, apparent separation follows fragments/composition/host taxonomy,
or ecological contrasts disappear under appropriate controls. A wide interval
or non-significant result is inconclusive, not proof of absence.

F2. A recurrent dark family represents an unrecognised compatible-solute
phosphatase, with conserved pathway context and a reproducible habitat association.

Candidate families: prioritise one putative mannosyl-3-phosphoglycerate
phosphatase family; trehalose-6-phosphate phosphatases are an alternative.
These are separate enzyme-family hypotheses and should not be pooled into
a single embedding cloud merely because they share an osmolyte annotation.

Precedent: Jiang et al. 2013 (10.1371/journal.pone.0077437) discovered and tested
a trehalose synthase from saline-alkali soil metagenomes. That is a precedent
for environmental compatible-solute enzyme discovery, not evidence assigning
our phosphatase candidates. Rao et al. 2006 (10.1110/ps.062096606) provides a
structural/context comparator for trehalose-phosphate phosphatases. Imminger
et al. 2024 (10.1038/s41467-024-46920-6) used genome-resolved metatranscriptomics
in a desert hydration/desiccation study; its solute-pathway observations supply
ecological motivation and a possible external expression dataset, subject to
accession, homolog specificity and raw-data feasibility checks.

Existing foothold: results/darkmatter_ecology/indicators/osmolyte_families.tsv
contains a historical 306-family candidate set with weak decoder assignments.
Example leads (identifiers only, pending current-catalogue remapping):
  1PRr2__self_SemiBin_81::1PRr2_1391604_4
  S52PRr1__single_SemiBin_53::S52PRr1_477345_9
The second row is attributed to Thermoplasmatota SW-10-69-26, offering a possible
connection to H1 if the mapping and function are corroborated. These are leads,
not validated enzymes. The historical group-level root enrichment was
inconclusive; its dN/dS values and decoder scores do not validate function.

Primary tests, in order: remap the leads to current families and assess coding
integrity; compare family membership against experimentally characterised
phosphatases and competing HAD-superfamily functions; test whether the family
recurs with a specific compatible-solute pathway more than with matched
same-lineage genes; only then test a preselected habitat contrast. An apparent
pathway gap must survive sensitive searches for alternative routes and genome
incompleteness. Do not select the environmental axis from whichever is strongest.

Evidence required: substrate-specific pocket and cap-domain conservation,
independent genomic-context support, repeat detection and raw-read support,
and preferably a homolog in an existing culture genome. A candidate's activity
and substrate specificity would require biochemical confirmation. External
transcript recruitment could support expression where homologs exist, while
remaining a distinct cohort and measurement from the EQ DNA samples.

Downgrade/stop: generic phosphatase similarities are equally compatible with
other substrates; no specific pathway context; current-catalogue remapping
fails; candidate loci are partial/chimeric; habitat effect is explained by
host abundance or batch. The first possible valuable outcome is a convincing
family assignment; a desert-adaptation claim requires additional evidence.

F3. EQ adds divergent rhodopsin lineages with conserved sensory-associated
genomic contexts, distinguishable from established energy-harvesting classes.

Precedent: Pushkarev et al. 2018 (10.1038/s41586-018-0225-9) discovered
heliorhodopsins using functional metagenomics and surveyed their environmental
distribution. Bulzu et al. 2021 (10.1128/msphere.00661-21) combined contextual,
domain-fusion and metatranscriptomic evidence for photosensory hypotheses;
Bulzu et al. 2022 (10.1128/msystems.00405-22) expanded rhodopsin diversity across
genomic/environmental collections. Tarlachkov et al. 2025
(10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2024.113083; final abstract verified, 2024 bioRxiv v1 full
text retained separately, final full text not obtained) already characterised diverse rhodopsins in desert-associated
Geodermatophilaceae. Therefore terrestrial occurrence alone has little novelty.

Existing foothold: RQ/D6/rhodopsin_hits.tsv is a historical PF01036/type-1
screen on the superseded 15,469-MAG basis. Its coarse compartment result was
null. It provides candidate identifiers, not current counts, and is incomplete
for heliorhodopsins and divergent family membership. The current paper also
records rhodopsin markers in some Halobacteriota.

Primary tests: quantify reference expansion after a current-catalogue search
including established rhodopsin classes; test recurrence of specific sensory
gene/domain associations against same-lineage neighbourhood controls. A
surface association is secondary and must be tested without assuming its sign.
Use membrane-protein controls for embeddings and confirm topology and
chromophore-binding architecture. Related seven-helix proteins are competing
assignments. Light sensing versus ion transport requires physiological or
biochemical evidence; genomic context establishes a functional hypothesis.

Downgrade/stop: all candidates fall within known environmental groups, context
associations track phylogeny, or distinct embeddings reflect membrane-protein
composition. Avoid predetermined percentages or p-values for expected hits.

F4. Ectoine synthase (EctC) diversity includes underrepresented EQ subfamilies
whose occurrence is associated with a defined compatible-solute pathway.

Precedent: Czech et al. 2019 (10.1038/s41598-018-36247-w) combines family-scale
genome surveys with structural and biochemical EctC characterisation, including
orphan EctC-like genes. Existing cupin-family diversity and known orphan loci
are essential comparators. This is a genome-survey/function-validation
precedent, rather than a direct multi-environment metagenomic discovery study.

Existing foothold: manuscript/main.tex and code/picrust2_bias_analysis.py
contain an ectoine marker panel. A current sequence-level candidate census has
not been established here.

Primary test: quantify added EctC diversity, separately for intact ectoine
pathways and orphan EctC-like loci, with competing cupin functions and host
taxonomy controlled. Test chemistry associations only once metadata permit a
well-defined exposure: XRF elemental ranks are not direct porewater salinity.

Evidence required: EctC-specific profile/active-site/context support and gene
integrity. Chemical product confirmation would establish EctC function;
sequence origin alone does not establish salt tolerance or industrial value.
Stop if findings are only additional close homologs or generic cupin hits.

F5. EQ expands a specific carbonic-anhydrase class with experimentally
testable biochemical properties.

Precedent: Pairoh et al. 2025 (10.1038/s41598-025-24713-1) used environmental
metagenomes and machine learning to prioritise gamma-carbonic anhydrases and
then measured activity and thermal properties. It did not validate all CA
classes or establish that structural similarity alone predicts activity.

Existing foothold: assembled proteins and genome context are available, but
no current family-specific candidate list was verified in this investigation.

Primary tests: choose beta or gamma CA as a separate family analysis after
checking feasibility; establish reference expansion with conserved catalytic
architecture and lineage/context support. Carbonic-anhydrase classes are
structurally distinct and must not be pooled into one novelty endpoint.
Activity and thermal/salt stability require direct measurements against
appropriate reference enzymes. No claim of thermostability follows from
desert provenance. This has a clearer applied validation route but a weaker
connection to the existing paper than F1/F2.

PROSTT5 FIGURE AND NOVELTY DESIGN (common to approved family tracks)

The intended endpoint is added family diversity relative to named, versioned
references. ProstT5 supplies a representation; the experiment does not add
novelty to the trained model itself.

1. Build a curated reference set including experimentally characterised members,
   environmental homologs, neighbouring subfamilies and plausible competing
   functions. Include SMAG/GEM and appropriate other environmental catalogues;
   use H3's lessons on depth, taxonomy, gene calling and reference composition.
   Query the entire current proteome for each chosen family, then label the
   original dark subset. Restricting discovery to dark candidates would bias
   the family comparison and hide known-to-dark continuity.
2. Establish coding integrity and family membership before inspecting t-SNE.
   Retain all quality strata and exclusions. Handle biological short proteins,
   fragments and domain fusions separately; use H4's full-chain/domain analyses
   to identify length/architecture effects.
3. Extract frozen ProstT5 embeddings with documented checkpoint, input mode,
   pooling, sequence handling and model provenance. Use the same representation
   for EQ and references. Protein/domain embedding choices are separate
   prespecified views. No model training is proposed at this stage.
4. Fit one joint t-SNE per family, retaining the same coordinates across panels
   coloured by EQ/reference origin, reference identity, host lineage, habitat,
   and evidence tier. Show experimentally supported reference anchors and any
   independently supported EQ candidates. Report seeds/perplexity sensitivity,
   neighbourhood preservation, and PCA/UMAP corroboration without choosing a
   projection on visual separation.
5. Quantify novelty in the original embedding space: reference-neighbour
   distances, reproducible neighbourhood coverage, added nonredundant clusters,
   and phylogenetic branch breadth. Calibrate against leave-one-clade-out known
   references and additions of equal numbers of external-soil proteins. Cluster
   definitions and family-specific thresholds must precede testing and include
   sensitivity analyses. Report full counts as well as balanced comparisons.
6. A prospective useful-resource endpoint is the reduction in nearest-reference
   distance for held-out EQ sites/lineages or independent desert homologs when
   adding discovery-set EQ sequences. Define holdouts before candidate selection;
   prior catalogue-wide exploration makes old samples internal validation,
   whereas untouched external data offer stronger independent evidence.
7. Require agreement with sequence/profile searches and supported phylogeny,
   plus coordinate-based checks for selected representatives. ProstT5 embeddings
   and ProstT5-predicted 3Di are related outputs, not two independent validations.
   Read support establishes DNA sequence reliability; culture-genome matches
   corroborate gene occurrence; RNA/protein measurements establish expression;
   biochemical measurements establish activity. Keep these evidence levels distinct.
8. Novelty is relative to pinned reference versions. Model-pretraining novelty
   requires checking both inherited ProtT5 training exposure and ProstT5's
   structure-training set where possible. A distant embedding point or current
   database no-hit does not establish absence from model training.

Precedent for this workflow: Wu et al. 2025
(10.1038/s41467-025-61599-z) used ProstT5 representations and a representation
tree to select PET hydrolases and performed biochemical validation. The retained
final text supports the representation-tree claim; it does not support citing
that paper as specifically demonstrating t-SNE novelty. PET hydrolases are an
additional applied option if the initial catalogue inspection supports them,
but their current EQ evidence is less specific than the leading options above.
Heinzinger et al. 2024 (10.1093/nargab/lqae150) documents ProstT5; Wattenberg et
al. 2016 (10.23915/distill.00002) explains limits of t-SNE interpretation.

Decision/stopping point
Author chooses the family tracks before new analysis. Recommended first stage:
bounded feasibility checks for F1 and F2, with F3 as a third candidate if desired.
Freeze family definitions, selection criteria and validation split before
large searches. Stop or downgrade when gene integrity or family assignment
fails. Present actual surviving candidates and results before committing to
new wet-lab validation or choosing the final manuscript example. Any GPU work
uses ORIX scheduling; CPU-heavy work uses IBEX Slurm workers. No heavy work
belongs on login nodes. Hydrogenases/CO dehydrogenases remain in lifeonair.
