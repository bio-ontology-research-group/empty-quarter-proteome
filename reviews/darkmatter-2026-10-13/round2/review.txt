# Neutral summary

The revision answers much of Round 1 by narrowing claims, and the narrowing is mostly honest:
- Selection evidence is restricted to the recurrent 12.5%, and the abstract no longer calls dark proteins accessory content.
- The structural control is described as positive-control retrieval.
- Rarefaction agreement is described as consistency.

Two new sequence-level analyses were run (IBEX jobs 53678755 and 53678768), plus pinned table-level checks. I independently verified two technical foundations:
- **MAG coordinate extraction is correct.** 82,869 of 82,874 extracted intervals translate to their recorded proteins. This holds across both strands, every Pyrodigal partial code (00/01/10/11) and the `_sub` representatives.
- **The structural tables reproduce from the raw Foldseek hits.** Recomputing per-model maxima from `pdb_hits.raw.tsv` and `afdb_hits.raw.tsv` gives the same 95 dark and 68 control alignment-normalised no-hits.

Four findings change interpretation, not just wording:

1. **The structural "control floor" mixes two failure modes.**
   - All 66 zero-score controls (of the 101 query-normalised control failures) are absent from both raw hit tables.
   - They are extended single helices: helix proxy 0.98; radius of gyration about 4.8 times the globular expectation. Retrieved controls look nothing like this.
   - These 66 models (2.2% of the 3,013 gate-passing controls) drive the alignment-normalised "ratio 1.38, CI crosses one" result.
   - Excluding zero-row models from both arms gives alignment-normalised no-hits of 2.6% (dark) versus 0.07% (controls).
2. **Two-thirds of the 1,031 whole-query no-hits are non-compact models.** Yet 77% of them (797) align at alnTM ≥ 0.5 over ≥100 residues covering at least half the query. "Whole-protein reference absence" is mostly "known region plus unmatched extension or arrangement" in non-globular models.
3. **The sign reversal under matching is an estimand effect.**
   - Matching keeps 7.5% of dark families with dS < 0.1 and 98.8% of those with dS 1–2.
   - 88% of matched families sit at dS 0.5–2.
   - Weighted to the dark arm's own distribution, the within-stratum difference is positive (+0.012); weighted to the control distribution it is negative (−0.022).
   - Within matched strata, dark families have about twice the control dN/dS at dS ≤ 0.25, and lower dN/dS above 0.5.
4. **The culture nucleotide test undercuts the protein-sharing contrast.** In the 8 pairs where the positive control works (≥90% recovery of protein-shared calls), 65.8% of protein-unmatched dark calls have their DNA in the cultured genome. For protein-unmatched annotated calls the figure is 25.3%. The manuscript omits the annotated-unmatched comparator altogether.

Separately:
- The Methods statement that rRNA/tRNA content "was not assessed" is false. A completed, registered analysis exists: only 126 of 5,438 representatives are MIMAG high quality.
- The environmental section, persistence statistic, taxonomy wording, Figs 1, 5 and 6, and data availability retain Round 1 problems.

**Strongest contribution after this round.** Under the current evidence, the defensible contribution is:
- the first genome-resolved, repeatedly sampled catalogue of a hyperarid sand sea;
- the 237-species Thermoplasmatota radiation, larger than GTDB's representation of those classes and associated with the subsurface;
- an explicitly controlled demonstration that each dark-proteome inference (constraint, sharing, structural novelty) depends on control construction and estimand.

The two potentially high-impact theses are not supported at present. "Dark proteins are strain-variable accessory content" is weakened by the new nucleotide data. "Dark families are under constraint comparable to known genes" is estimand-dependent. That is publishable in Nature Communications only if the controls are presented correctly (R2-02 to R2-05) and the paper is framed as a resource plus a controlled census.

## R2-02. Critical for the structural section. [RAW and RECOMPUTED] The 66 zero-score controls are a Foldseek failure on extended helices, not a retrieval floor. This reverses the alignment-normalised inference.

**Manuscript:**
- "The query-score no-hit groups include 22 dark models and 66 controls with zero reported scores, leaving 1{,}009 and 35 with nonzero scores below 0.5."
- "Scoring the same alignments by aligned length ... leaves 95 candidates (3.35\%) against 2.26\% of controls, and standardised on confidence and length the ratio is 1.38 with an interval (0.87--2.41) that crosses one."
- Discussion: "while local similarity is detected much more frequently."

**Evidence:**
- **Integration code.** `05_integrate.py best_scores()` assigns [0,0,0] to any query without a row. `round2_checks.py` defines zero as `(q==0)&(a==0)`.
- **The 66 controls have no rows at all [RAW].** I scanned both raw hit files (65 MB PDB, 121 MB AFDB). None of the 66 IDs occurs in either. The 3,570 control IDs that do occur include self-hits; for example KF001595 matches PDB entry 5da8 at qTM 0.92.
- **They are extended helices [RAW, coordinate probe].** I parsed the model PDB files via `query_pdb` symlinks. The helix proxy is the fraction of CA(i, i+3) distances under 6 Å. The globular Rg expectation is 2.2·N^0.38 Å.

| Set | n | Mean helix proxy | Share > 0.8 helical | Mean Rg / expected | Share > 1.5× |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-score controls | 66 | 0.98 | 66/66 | 4.79 | 66/66 |
| Random retrieved controls | 66 | 0.50 | 11% | 1.23 | 15% |
| Zero-score dark models | 22 | 0.74 | 64% | 3.43 | 22/22 |

- **Not a shard artefact.** Their maximum absolute coordinate is 57–188 Å, versus a median of 36 Å for retrieved controls. Control-fold shards are evenly mixed across the two groups.

**Consequences:**
- The floor mixes "extended helix that Foldseek returns nothing for" with real near-threshold misses. The same 66 models are all but 2 of the 68 alignment-normalised control failures.
- Excluding zero-row models from both arms [RECOMPUTED]:
  - Query-normalised no-hits: 1,009/2,816 (35.8%) dark vs 35/2,947 (1.19%) controls.
  - Alignment-normalised no-hits: 73/2,816 (2.6%) dark vs 2/2,947 (0.07%) controls.
- So the "crosses one" result for local structure is an artefact of the control composition, not evidence that dark models are locally as retrievable as known folds.
- The 22 zero-score dark models are non-globular helical models, not novel folds.
- Uncertainty: I did not run Foldseek. The most plausible mechanism is prefilter failure on low-information helical 3Di strings (the Foldseek paper notes low information density outside conserved cores), but I have not established it. Self-retrieval still makes the remaining control floor a weak calibration (R1-09a remains open).

**Smallest remedy:**
- Define an outcome-blind geometric class before scoring: for example DSSP helix fraction ≥0.8 with Rg ratio ≥1.5, or zero-row status.
- Report both arms with and without it.
- Drop or recompute the standardised alignment-normalised ratio on the eligible subset.
- Revise the Results sentence and the Discussion "local similarity" sentence accordingly.
- Remove the 22 dark helices from the "candidate" language.
- Optionally confirm the mechanism by re-searching 5 of these controls with prefilter masking off or exhaustive search (minutes of CPU).

## R2-03. Major. [RECOMPUTED, PLAUSIBLE mechanism] The 1,031 whole-query no-hits are dominated by non-compact models with gated local matches

**Manuscript:**
- "The 1{,}031 families are candidates for whole-protein reference absence under the stated metric."
- Discussion: "36.3\% of confident models fall below the whole-query similarity threshold".

**Evidence:**
- Rg above 1.5 times the globular expectation:
  - 682/1,031 K models (66.1%);
  - 39.3% of 600 randomly sampled confident non-K dark models;
  - 9.8% of 600 sampled controls.
- Gated alignment-normalised scores, recomputed from raw hits:
  - Requiring ≥50 aligned residues barely changes the alignment-normalised no-hit count (97 vs 95). The "96.7% contain a region that aligns" claim is robust to that gate, which partly closes R1-09d.
  - Under ≥100 aligned residues and query coverage ≥0.5, 797 of the 1,031 still have alnTM ≥0.5.
  - The median aligned length of the best alnTM hit is 172 residues, against a median query length of 265.
- Query-length normalisation penalises multi-domain or extended models. ESMFold inter-domain placement is not validated here.
- The best qTM and best alnTM are independent maxima, possibly from different targets (`best_scores`). This is not disclosed.
- Orr & Bateman 2026 (retained full text, DOI-verified) report that ESMFold assigns unexpectedly high pLDDT to short spurious sequences, with discrimination improving above 100 aa. This supports keeping the length gate, not treating confidence as validation.

**Remedy (choose one):**
- (a) Run domain segmentation (Merizo or Chainsaw; both are in the retained literature, and CPU is feasible for 1,031 models). Classify each model as all-domains-matched, ≥1 confident unmatched domain, or unmatched low-confidence/linker region. Report pTM or PAE where available.
- (b) Demote the 1,031 to "models whose full length is not matched". Report the compactness and gated alnTM numbers beside them, and remove "whole-protein reference absence" from the Discussion headline.

Also disclose that the maxima are taken independently.

## R2-04. Major. [RECOMPUTED] The matched dN/dS "reversal" is an estimand and divergence-range effect; presentation and uncertainty are insufficient

**Manuscript:**
- "In this common-support subset, median dN/dS was 0.386 for dark families and 0.423 for annotated families. The median difference ranged from $-0.0382$ to $-0.0358$ across 20 seeded selections. ... The reversal from the pooled comparison identifies cohort composition as a material determinant of the between-group contrast."

**Evidence** (`matched_families.tsv`, `family_covariates.tsv`, `dnds.tsv`):
- Share of dark families retained, by mean-dS stratum:

| dS stratum | Retained | Share |
|---|---|---|
| [0, 0.1) | 686/9,194 | 7.5% |
| [0.1, 0.25) | 941/7,419 | 12.7% |
| [0.25, 0.5) | 5,492/17,873 | 30.7% |
| [0.5, 1) | 41,340/49,588 | 83.4% |
| [1, 2] | 12,968/13,126 | 98.8% |

- Overall, 88.4% of matched families have dS 0.5–2, a range where pairwise Nei–Gojobori estimates approach saturation.
- Matched-set medians (dark vs control) by finer dS bin:
  - (0, 0.05]: 0.715 vs 0.367
  - (0.05, 0.1]: 0.692 vs 0.305
  - (0.1, 0.25]: 0.593 vs 0.333
  - (0.25, 0.5]: 0.484 vs 0.461
  - (0.5, 0.75]: 0.467 vs 0.506, with n 25,717 vs 19,903
  - (0.75, 1]: 0.370 vs 0.431, with n 15,617 vs 21,433
  - (1, 1.5]: 0.265 vs 0.321
- The residual dS imbalance (SMD −0.124) comes from the wide 0.5–1 bin, where dark families concentrate in its lower half.
- Weighted mean of within-stratum differences across the 553 common strata:
  - weighted to the dark arm: +0.012;
  - weighted to the control arm: −0.022;
  - with matched-n weights: −0.018.
  - So the sign depends on the target population.
- The 20-seed range only reflects which larger-arm families are drawn within fixed strata. It is not sampling uncertainty.
- The matching covariate `mean_dS` is estimated from the same pairs as the outcome, so it shares estimation noise with dN/dS. The claim "dN/dS is never a matching input" is literally true but not the whole story.
- Literature (retained, DOI-verified full texts) supports reading the low-dS strata cautiously, in both directions:
  - Kryazhimskiy & Plotkin 2008: within-population dN/dS is "relatively insensitive to selection pressure".
  - Mugal et al. 2014: dN/dS is time-dependent.
  - Neither is cited in the manuscript.

**Remedy:**
- Make stratum-specific contrasts primary, with a family-level bootstrap CI per stratum and n per stratum in Fig. 2c.
- State the estimand of the matched comparison: control-like divergence and composition.
- Add the dark-weighted estimate.
- Replace "reversal" with "the direction depends on divergence: dark families have higher dN/dS at dS < 0.25 and lower at dS > 0.5."
- Optionally match on `mean_pid` or a split-pair dS to remove shared noise.
- Cite the time-dependence and population-genetics literature.
- None of this needs new sequence computation.

## R2-05. Major. [RECOMPUTED, CONFIRMED omission] The culture nucleotide check is technically sound, but its reported subset hides that most protein-unmatched dark calls in close pairs are present as DNA

**Manuscript:**
- "A nucleotide-level sensitivity search found a high-identity alignment covering at least 90\% of the coding interval for 347 of 1{,}105 eligible dark calls without a reciprocal protein hit (31.4\%). The same search recovered 172 of 236 eligible dark calls with a protein hit (72.9\%) and 47{,}532 of 65{,}477 annotated calls with a protein hit (72.6\%). DNA support for some protein-unmatched calls implicates differences in gene prediction ..."
- Methods: "we extracted each MAG gene's recorded interval from its original representative assembly".

**Positive verification [RAW]:**
- Extracted intervals translate to the recorded MAG proteins for 82,869/82,874 genes.
- This holds for + and − strands, every partial code, and the `_sub` representatives.
- Bounds are asserted in code, and minimap2 searches both strands, so not reverse-complementing is harmless.

**Problems:**

(a) **The fourth cell is omitted.** Annotated calls without a protein hit have DNA support in 2,611/16,056 (16.3%).

(b) **The positive control's shortfall is an identity-threshold effect, not minimap2 insensitivity.**
- 18,009 protein-shared calls lack DNA support.
- 16,769 of them still have ≥90% coverage, with median best identity 0.897.
- Only 65 have no alignment.
- With identity relaxed to ≥0.85: shared calls 91.6%, dark-unmatched 40.8%, annotated-unmatched 25.6%.

(c) **Pair heterogeneity is extreme.** Positive-control recovery per pair ranges from 6.1% to 97.6%. In three pairs it is below 15%, so the test is uninformative there. In the 8 pairs with recovery ≥90%:

| Group | DNA-supported | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Dark, protein-unmatched | 185/281 | 65.8% |
| Annotated, protein-unmatched | 640/2,527 | 25.3% |
| Dark, protein-matched | 111/125 | 88.8% |
| Annotated, protein-matched | 27,865/29,154 | 95.6% |

(d) **Effect on Fig. 2d.** In near-identical genomes, most "unshared" dark calls exist at DNA level but lack a Bakta protein partner. This happens far more often than for annotated calls. So the 80.1% vs 15.8% protein-sharing contrast is largely a gene-call or protein-match phenomenon specific to dark ORFs, not strain-variable content. Bakta's sORF/spurious-ORF filtering makes this directly relevant to coding status (R2-06).

(e) **Methods wording.** The intervals come from the dereplicated representative FASTA (`05_refine/_drep/out/dereplicated_genomes/{rep}.fa`), not "original representative assembly". minimap2 is a local "2.17-r943-dirty" build at `/home/hohndor/bin`; record it as such.

**Remedy:**
- Report all four cells, per-pair positive-control recovery, and the results restricted to pairs with recovery ≥90% (pre-declared). These are already computed in `per_gene.tsv`.
- For the DNA-supported unmatched dark calls, intersect the culture alignment locus with the culture Bakta GFF (coordinate join, minutes). Classify each as:
  - no Bakta CDS;
  - Bakta CDS in the same frame (protein-match failure);
  - CDS in another frame or on the opposite strand (shadow candidate).
- Rewrite the Fig. 2d caption and Results to state that protein-level sharing understates DNA-level sharing for dark calls.
- Harmonised gene calling stays desirable but is no longer needed to show the problem.

## R2-06. Major. [still open from R1-03] No shadow-ORF or overlap screen, although the evidence now points at gene-call artefacts

**Smallest remedy:**

- (ii) Run AntiFam `hmmsearch --cut_ga` on 536k proteins (CPU-hours).
- Carry a family-level flag into the selection statistics.
- Both steps are cheap and decisive for the "frame-dependent constraint" sentence in the abstract.

# Reviewer uncertainty

- The helix and compactness classification is a heuristic: CA(i, i+3) under 6 Å as helix proxy; Rg expectation 2.2·N^0.38 Å.
  - It covers all 66 zero-score controls, all 22 zero-score dark models and all 1,031 K models.
  - Q-not-K and control comparators are random samples of 600 (fixed shuffle).
  - The Foldseek mechanism is a hypothesis.
- The ATT/ATC numbers are weighted means of within-stratum mean differences of family medians. They are not a formal estimator with CIs.
- The persistence null is an analytic approximation, not a permutation on the real matrix.
- I did not verify the ecology, PICRUSt2, BGC, AMR or differential-function numbers, or the Thermoplasmatota depth statistics.
- Pairs with recovery ≥90% (8 of 22) were selected by me after seeing the data. Root should pre-declare the criterion.
