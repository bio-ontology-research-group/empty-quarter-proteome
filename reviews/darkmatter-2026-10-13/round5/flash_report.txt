### Hypothesis 1: Structural Query-Score Deficits Reflect Domain Coverage Ratios Rather Than Novel Fold Topologies

#### 1. Scientific Question & Interpretation
Does the 36.3% rate of confident dark models falling below whole-query TM-score 0.5 against PDB/AFDB indicate novel three-dimensional folds, or is it a mathematical artifact of sub-query alignment coverage in multi-domain proteins with familiar folds?
- **Positive Outcome (Artifact established)**: Dark models with $TM_{\mathrm{query}} < 0.5$ have high alignment TM-scores ($TM_{\mathrm{aln}} \ge 0.70$) that scale inversely with query length ($L_{\mathrm{aln}}/L_{\mathrm{query}}$ ratio decays), and their failure rate drops to control levels when evaluated on aligned domains. **Meaning**: Desert dark proteins are not topologically novel folds; they are known structural domains flanked by unaligned terminal regions or distinct multi-domain arrangements.
- **Informative Negative Outcome (True fold novelty)**: A distinct cluster of models maintains $TM_{\mathrm{aln}} < 0.5$ and $TM_{\mathrm{query}} < 0.5$ even under full-length coverage ($\ge 80\%$). **Meaning**: Identification of a bounded, genuine candidate set of novel structural folds.

### Hypothesis 2: DNA-Supported Culture No-CDS Discrepancies Persist in Complete Long Calls and Reflect Annotation Pipeline Filtering

#### 1. Scientific Question & Interpretation
In paired MAG–culture comparisons, 344 of 735 DNA-supported dark calls have no overlapping Bakta CDS in the cultured isolate assembly. Does this discrepancy persist among complete, long genes ($\ge 100$ aa) and recurrent family members, or is it an artifact of short/fragmentary gene predictions in MAGs? And does re-calling the cultured assemblies with Prodigal/Pyrodigal recover these unannotated ORFs?
- **Positive Outcome (Caller-choice established)**: Discrepancies persist at ~40% in complete long calls and recurrent families, but running Pyrodigal on the culture assemblies calls the identical ORFs. **Meaning**: A substantial fraction of "uncultivated dark proteome" exists in cultured isolates but is systematically filtered out by isolate annotation pipelines (e.g. Bakta's heuristic length/overlap thresholds or Aragorn/tRNA shadow masking).
- **Informative Negative Outcome (Biological/Sequence Divergence)**: Discrepancies disappear in complete long calls (restricted entirely to short partial genes), OR Pyrodigal also rejects the ORFs in the culture sequence. **Meaning**: MAG dark predictions are either short false-positive calls or disrupted by indels/pseudogenisation in cultured strains.

#### 2. Exact Available Input Files & Unit of Replication
- **Unit of Replication**: The individual MAG gene call ($n = 1,341$ eligible dark calls, $n = 81,533$ eligible annotated calls across 22 representative–culture pairs).
- **Files**:

- Odds ratio of `no_major_CDS_overlap` between dark and annotated calls.

### Hypothesis 3: Repeated Expedition Detection Does Not Exceed Spatial Prevalence and Sampling-Design Expectations

#### 1. Scientific Question & Interpretation
The manuscript reports that 79.4% of dark families are detected in all 5 expeditions (by read mapping) and 28.9% are assembled in all 5. Does this multi-trip detection demonstrate genuine seasonal persistence / temporal stability, or is it statistically indistinguishable from a null model conditioning on spatial prevalence and the unbalanced expedition sampling design?
- **Positive Outcome (Spatial sampling null sufficient)**: When conditioning on family prevalence ($k$ samples) and the site visitation network (Trip 2 visiting only 7 sites), observed 5-trip detection matches or is lower than null expectations. **Meaning**: Detection across 5 trips is a mathematical consequence of high spatial occupancy across the transect, not evidence of temporal stability across seasonal visits.
- **Informative Negative Outcome (True temporal persistence)**: Families with low spatial prevalence ($k < 20$) appear in all 5 trips significantly more often than expected by chance ($p < 0.01$). **Meaning**: Specific dark families exhibit persistent temporal recurrence at localized sites across multiple years.

#### 2. Exact Available Input Files & Unit of Replication
- **Unit of Replication**: The dark protein family ($n = 106,042$ families).
- **Files**:

  - Remote IBEX: `/ibex/scratch/projects/c2014/rob/metagenomics/06_darkmatter/darkfam_basis_clean/results/fam_presence.tsv` (106,042 families $\times$ 551 samples).
  - Remote IBEX: `/ibex/scratch/projects/c2014/rob/metagenomics/06_darkmatter/darkfam_basis_clean/results/fam_recurrence.tsv`.

#### 3. Primary Effect / Estimand
- Observed vs. expected probability of all-5 expedition detection: $\Delta P = P_{\mathrm{obs}}(\text{5 trips} \mid k) - P_{\mathrm{null}}(\text{5 trips} \mid k)$.
- Fraction of families exceeding 95th percentile of site-stratified permutation null.

#### 4. Covariates, Controls & Dependence

### Hypothesis 4: Thermoplasmatota Darkness Reflects Reference-Cataloguing Proximity (AAI Gradient) Rather Than Bin Fragmentation

#### 1. Scientific Question & Interpretation
Thermoplasmatota genomes exhibit an elevated median dark fraction (31.0% vs. 4.8% in matched archaea and 6.9% in matched bacteria). Does this elevated darkness persist in strictly near-complete bins ($\ge 90\%$ completeness, $\le 5\%$ contamination), and does within-lineage variation in darkness decay as phylogenetic distance to known references (AAI) decreases?
- **Positive Outcome (Cataloguing gap confirmed)**: Darkness remains ~30% in near-complete bins, but within Thermoplasmatota, dark fraction is negatively correlated with AAI to the nearest GTDB R232 / MGnify comparator. Genomes reaching the genus-level heuristic ($\mathrm{AAI} \ge 65\%$) have significantly lower dark fractions than isolated lineages ($\mathrm{AAI} < 55\%$). **Meaning**: Thermoplasmatota darkness is a consequence of deep phylogenetic placement outside existing reference databases, not an abnormal biological rate of proteome turnover or binning fragmentation.
- **Informative Negative Outcome (Uniform biological darkness)**: Dark fraction remains strictly constant at ~31% regardless of AAI to nearest references (uncoupled from phylogenetic distance). **Meaning**: High darkness is a lineage-wide intrinsic trait across both deep and relatively characterized Thermoplasmatota clades.

#### 2. Exact Available Input Files & Unit of Replication

- **Files**:

### Hypothesis 5: PICRUSt2 Functional Saturation Is Driven by Low-Abundance Ancestral State Leakage

#### 1. Scientific Question & Interpretation
PICRUSt2 emits all 10,543 reference KOs for 93.5% of samples under the default presence rule ($\text{abundance} > 0$), resulting in high nominal recall (86.0%) but 3,078 predictor-only false positives per sample. Does imposing an abundance threshold (e.g. relative abundance $> 10^{-5}$ or copy-number cutoff) break saturation, restore specificity, and align functional diversity with metagenomic reality?
- **Positive Outcome (Threshold sensitivity demonstrated)**: Predicted KO richness falls from 10,543 to the biologically plausible range (7,000–9,000 KOs), Jaccard similarity peaks at an optimal threshold, and false-positive KOs are eliminated. **Meaning**: The apparent saturation of amplicon functional prediction is a thresholding artifact; calibrated cutoffs provide realistic functional profiles.
- **Informative Negative Outcome (Persistent saturation)**: Saturation persists even under aggressive cutoffs ($> 10^{-3}$), or recall drops catastrophically before false positives are suppressed. **Meaning**: In hyperarid biomes with high NSTI (median 0.159), ancestral state reconstruction diffuses counts broadly across the entire KEGG ontology, rendering presence/absence calls fundamentally uninformative regardless of filtering.

#### 2. Exact Available Input Files & Unit of Replication
- **Unit of Replication**: The metagenomic sample ($n = 448$ samples with both CoverM MAG profile and 16S PICRUSt2 profile).
- **Files**:
  - Remote IBEX: `/ibex/scratch/projects/c2014/rob/metagenomics/rebuild_20260729/picrust2_current_20260803/inputs/ko_metagenome_unstrat.tsv` (10,543 KOs $\times$ 1,270 profiles, pinned checksum `c71c2256...`).
  - Remote IBEX: `/ibex/scratch/projects/c2014/rob/metagenomics/06_reference_bias/refbias_clean/results/mag_ko_relab_by_sample.tsv.gz`.
  - Compact: [`reproducibility/darkmatter/inputs/06_reference_bias/refbias_clean/results/per_sample_comparison.tsv.gz`](file:///home/leechuck/Documents/papers/empty-quarter-mags/reproducibility/darkmatter/inputs/06_reference_bias/refbias_clean/results/per_sample_comparison.tsv.gz).

#### 3. Primary Effect / Estimand
- Median predicted KO richness, recall of MAG-encoded KOs, Jaccard index, and false-positive count across a ladder of abundance cutoffs ($\tau \in \{0, 10^{-6}, 10^{-5}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}\}$).

#### 4. Covariates, Controls & Dependence
- **Covariates**: Weighted NSTI, 16S sequencing depth, sample soil position (Surface vs Deep).
- **Controls**: MAG-encoded KOs from refined bins as metagenomic ground truth.
- **Dependence**: Paired samples from the same site.

#### 5. Pitfalls, Artifacts & Leakage
- MAGs themselves do not capture 100% of the community; "false positives" include unbinned genes.
- PICRUSt2 predictions use normalized counts; cutoffs must account for sequencing depth variation.

#### 6. Realistic Implementation
