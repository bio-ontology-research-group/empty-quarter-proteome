The manuscript titled "Known phyla, unknown proteome: a controlled census of the Rub' al-Khali soil metagenome" presents a large-scale metagenomic assembly, binning, and functional/structural audit of 551 soil and root-zone samples across a 1,000 km transect of the Rub' al-Khali desert, yielding 17,677 bins dereplicated into 5,438 species-level metagenome-assembled genome (MAG) representatives and a 132.4-million-call protein catalogue. Rather than claiming novel phyla or radical fold space, the manuscript makes a disciplined, negative-control-backed argument: (i) all 5,438 species representatives fall within 31 known GTDB r232 phyla even though 77.5% lack a species assignment; (ii) 4,292,446 non-redundant proteins (7.5% of the MAG-assigned catalogue) lack sequence homologues in KEGG, eggNOG, Bakta, Pfam, and UniRef50, and 106,042 recurrent multi-species dark families show purifying selection (median dN/dS = 0.4253, 96.7% with dN/dS < 1) after controlling for synonymous divergence, gene length, GC content, family size, AntiFam hits, and opposite-strand overlaps; (iii) 722 co-located cultured isolates carry slightly lower per-genome dark fractions than conspecific MAGs, and nucleotide-level alignment plus Bakta locus audits show that roughly half of DNA-supported MAG dark calls at 99.7% ANI reflect gene-calling differences rather than absent DNA; (iv) Thermoplasmatota (237 species, 235 in Poseidoniales) carry 31.0% dark proteins per genome and a 4.6-fold enrichment of lineage-specific recurrent dark families; (v) ESMFold and Foldseek screening against PDB and AlphaFold DB shows that apparent fold novelty among high-confidence dark models collapses from 36.3% under query-length normalization to 3.3% (and 2.59% among models with retrieved Foldseek hits) under alignment-length normalization because most query-score failures are multi-domain proteins with high-similarity local domain hits; and (vi) an explicit amplicon-to-MAG audit on 448 paired samples quantifies the reference-database ceiling of 16S-based functional prediction (PICRUSt2) and shows via binomial rarefaction that residual sequence novelty against SMAG, MGnify, GEM, and SPIRE reflects biome-specific sampling rather than catalogue size alone.

Independent computational verification of the Round 4 revision confirms that the authors have substantively and transparently resolved the major technical concerns raised in Rounds 1 through 3. Running reproducibility/darkmatter/current_revision_checks.py and reproducibility/darkmatter/check_current_tables.py against the frozen compressed inputs and derived audit tables verified all 49 current revision assertions with zero failures. 1. [Major - Demonstrated statistical/methodological artifact in Results lines 352-358, Methods lines 709-718, and Figure 5a] PICRUSt2 "86.0% presence-recall" is driven by trivial near-saturation of the entire 10,543-KO PICRUSt2 reference set when presence is defined as predicted abundance > 0.
Evidence:
In Results lines 352-358, the manuscript reports that across 448 paired samples, the MAG catalogue encodes a median of 8,666.5 KOs per sample while PICRUSt2 predicts a median of 10,543 KOs, and states: "Of the MAG KOs in a sample, a median of 1,200 (13.97%, IQR 13.79-14.11%) lie outside PICRUSt2's 10,543-KO reference set and cannot be predicted regardless of 16S resolution, whereas 0 (IQR 0-0%) are in the reference set but missed, giving a median presence-recall of 86.0% (IQR 85.9-86.2%)."
Direct inspection of reproducibility/darkmatter/inputs/06_reference_bias/refbias_clean/results/per_sample_comparison.tsv.gz (n = 448 samples) and reproducibility/darkmatter/upstream_code/06_reference_bias/refbias_clean/scripts/refbias_3_compare.py (lines 48-55) reveals why mag_in_ref_not_predicted is 0 in almost every sample:
- Across the 448 samples, n_pic_ko (the number of KOs predicted by PICRUSt2 with abundance > 0) has Q25 = 10,543, median = 10,543, Q75 = 10,543, and max = 10,543. Specifically, 419 of 448 samples (93.5%) emit all 10,543 KOs in PICRUSt2's entire reference universe with nonzero abundance (and 438 of 448 emit >= 10,500 KOs; minimum = 9,683).
- Consequently, in 420 of 448 samples (93.8%), mag_in_ref_not_predicted is identically 0, and pic_recall is algebraically equal to 1 - frac_mag_outside_reference (median 86.03%, IQR 85.89-86.21%).
- At the same > 0 threshold, PICRUSt2 predicts a median of 3,077.5 KOs per sample (column pic_only, IQR 2,850.25-3,394.25; 29.2% of all predicted KOs) that are absent from the sample's MAGs, and the median Jaccard similarity between MAG KOs and PICRUSt2 KOs is 0.634 (IQR 0.608-0.652).
Scientific consequence:
Because any ASV in a rich soil community contributing even a fractional copy number (> 0) marks a KO as "present" in refbias_3_compare.py, PICRUSt2 predicts virtually 100% of its 10,543-KO database in almost every sample. Reporting that "0 (IQR 0-0%) are in the reference set but missed, giving a median presence-recall of 86.0%" without stating that PICRUSt2 predicts all 10,543 reference KOs in 93.5% of samples (with ~3,078 false-positive KOs per sample) misleads readers into thinking PICRUSt2 has high sample-level sensitivity, when in reality within-reference specificity is zero at an unthresholded (> 0) presence cutoff and "86.0% recall" is simply the complement of the 13.97% out-of-reference ceiling.
Feasible correction:
Rewrite Results lines 352-358 (and the Figure 5a caption) to state explicitly that at the unthresholded (> 0) presence definition, PICRUSt2 predicts all 10,543 reference KOs in 419 of 448 samples (generating a median of 3,077.5 predicted KOs per sample not found in that sample's MAGs, median Jaccard = 0.634), so that the 86.0% recall is entirely a reflection of reference-set overlap (86.03% of sample MAG KOs reside in the 10,543-KO reference) rather than selective recovery of sample-specific pathways.
