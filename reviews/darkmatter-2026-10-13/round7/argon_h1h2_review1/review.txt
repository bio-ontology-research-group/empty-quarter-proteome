The manuscript titled "Known phyla, unknown proteome: a controlled census of the Rub' al-Khali soil metagenome" presents a metagenomic survey of 551 soil samples from 61 locations across the Rub' al-Khali desert, recovering 14,426 refined bins dereplicated at 95% average nucleotide identity into 5,438 species-level genome representatives across 51 GTDB release 232 phyla, alongside 43,423,322 genome-linked protein cluster representatives (of which 4,292,446, or 9.89%, lack a UniRef50 match, forming 106,042 recurrent dark families) and 2,838 confidence-qualified monomer ESMFold models (mean pLDDT at least 0.70, length at least 100 residues). Neither analysis provides a standalone mechanistic discovery that elevates the manuscript beyond a controlled metagenomic census, and neither warrants a new main-text figure. Instead, both serve as bounded conditioning controls: H1 shows that across the 237 desert Thermoplasmatota species representatives, per-genome dark fraction exhibits strong residual phylogenetic covariance under a Brownian-motion PGLS model (fitted Pagel's lambda = 0.915784) alongside opposite-signed conditional partial associations with proteome-wide amino-acid identity (AAI) and reciprocal-best-hit (RBH) gene-content overlap to nearest GTDB/MGnify relatives, while H2 shows that among the 353 multi-species families passing the primary 4-flank complete-window gate, both low-query-TM (K) and fold-matched (Q\K) arms exceed same-contig positional expectations for flanking KO recurrence, but do not differ significantly from one another once conditioned on species breadth, coarse host taxonomy, and flanking KO annotation opportunity. 3. H1 methodological bounds: residual phylogenetic covariance, non-disjoint conditional associations, environmental MAG comparators, and dual gene-caller basis.
Four substantive bounds govern the interpretation of H1:
First, the n = 237 representatives constitute the complete enumerated cohort of dereplicated Rub' al-Khali Thermoplasmatota species in this catalogue. Accordingly, the Student-t confidence intervals (df = 230) and two-sided p-values in S1 through S7 are model-based quantities conditional on the selected GTDB-Tk r232 ar53 FastTree topology and branch lengths (thermo_tree.nwk) and on treated-as-fixed Pagel's lambda = 0.915784. Across the 95% profile-likelihood interval for lambda ([0.846303, 0.957823]), the union of conditional 95% Student-t intervals is [+0.000758, +0.002550] for beta_AAI (point estimates +0.001790 at lambda = 0.846303 to +0.001613 at lambda = 0.957823) and [-0.178736, -0.095753] for beta_RBH (point estimates -0.139789 to -0.133697; round6/snapshots/h1_final/results/v3_pgls_lambda_profile.tsv). While midpoint and explicit 30-outgroup rooting produce identical 237-tip variance-covariance matrices (results/v3_phylo_vcv_validation.json) and unnormalized Brownian VCV scaling gives nearly identical estimates (S2: lambda_ML = 0.916132, beta_AAI = +0.001744 +/- 0.000407, p = 2.69e-5; beta_RBH = -0.140505 +/- 0.019533, p = 8.83e-12), phylogenetic topology and branch-length uncertainty are not propagated. Crucially, fitted lambda = 0.915784 quantifies residual phylogenetic covariance under the Brownian-motion model, not a percentage of variance explained in darkness and not proof that vertical lineage history dominates all other evolutionary or ecological causes.
Second, the positive partial slope on nearest-relative AAI (+0.001704) and negative partial slope on RBH gene-content overlap (-0.137484) are conditional associations within the multivariable model (holding log10 genome length, log10 N50, completeness, and contamination constant) and remain directionally consistent across all sensitivity specifications: S3 using the shared catalogue denominator for RBH (n = 237, beta_AAI = +0.001693 +/- 0.000407, p = 4.50e-5; beta_RBH = -0.136591 +/- 0.019416, p = 2.27e-11), S4 restricted to c__SW-10-69-26 (n = 185, beta_AAI = +0.001921 +/- 0.000491, p = 1.29e-4; beta_RBH = -0.180441 +/- 0.022624, p = 1.78e-13), S5 omitting dominant family f__JAJPHT01 (n = 97, beta_AAI = +0.001494 +/- 0.000513, p = 4.53e-3; beta_RBH = -0.102379 +/- 0.030547, p = 1.18e-3), S6 restricted to same-class comparators in c__SW-10-69-26 and c__E2 (n = 236, beta_AAI = +0.001568 +/- 0.000392, p = 8.39e-5; beta_RBH = -0.145879 +/- 0.019191, p = 7.49e-13), and S7 adding the CheckM2 partial=00 complete-gene fraction proxy (n = 232, beta_AAI = +0.001550 +/- 0.000452, p = 7.25e-4; beta_RBH = -0.104756 +/- 0.026891, p = 1.29e-4). However, as documented in round6/snapshots/h1_final/DOSSIER.md (Section 6.2) and h2_v2/H1_COMPLETENESS.md, N_dark (catalogue genes lacking UniRef50/functional hits on 90%-identity cluster representatives) and N_RBH (single-genome Pyrodigal calls with a DIAMOND reciprocal best hit to the nearest external GTDB/MGnify reference genome) are derived from different gene callers and non-mutually-exclusive criteria: an unannotated protein in a desert MAG can also have an uncharacterized RBH partner in an external environmental MAG. Thus N_dark and N_RBH do not form a disjoint core-versus-accessory partition, and the positive conditional AAI slope must not be interpreted as a positive marginal correlation or as evidence of accessory genome expansion, horizontal gene transfer, or de novo gene birth.
Third, the 237 genome-comparator pairs map to 32 unique external reference accessions (229 pairs using GTDB r232 species representatives and 8 pairs using MGnify genomes; 142 labeled soil, 3 marine sediment, and 92 GTDB-only accessions lacking habitat metadata; results/v3_comparator_metadata_summary.tsv). None of the comparators are verified cultivated isolates, empirical AAI spans 48.678% to 80.041% (median 64.774%; 50.389% to 80.041% across the 236 c__SW-10-69-26 and c__E2 genomes), and pair counts must not be presented as independent comparator genomes.
Fourth, the primary response (dark_fraction = N_dark / N_proteins_cat, median 3,302.0 proteins) and primary RBH predictor (frac_rbh_desert = aai_n_rbh / n_prot_aai, median 3,312.0 proteins; difference n_prot_aai - n_proteins_cat ranging from -13 to +93 proteins, median +18.0, Pearson r = 0.999752) use different gene-call bases, and five genomes (48PRr1__SemiBin_112, 48PRr1__SemiBin_141, 48PRr1__SemiBin_510, 48PRr1__SemiBin_539, and 48PRr1__SemiBin_849; results/v3_closure_verification.json) lack archived CheckM2 .faa files on IBEX and are omitted in S7 (n = 232).

4. H2 complete-window attrition (75.84% loss in Tier 1), differential survival between K and Q\K, and unadjusted partial-window confounding in Tier 3.
In H2 (reviews/darkmatter-2026-10-13/round7/snapshots/h2_initial and h2_v2), filtering the 2,838 confidence-qualified monomer structural dark families (1,031 in low-query-TM K with qTM < 0.5; 1,807 in fold-matched Q\K with qTM >= 0.5) to families present on refined MAG contigs across at least 2 dRep 95% ANI species clusters (Gate 3) retains 1,461 families (570 K, 891 Q\K). Enforcing the primary Tier 1 complete-window gate (requiring at least 2 distinct species whose primary locus has all 4 flanking genes m2, m1, p1, p2 present on the contig and mapped by exact coordinates to Bakta/eggNOG) excludes 1,108 of the 1,461 families (75.84% attrition, coded -1), leaving 353 evaluable families (24.16%). Moreover, Tier 1 family survival is significantly higher in K (173/570 = 30.35%) than in Q\K (180/891 = 20.20%; two-sided Fisher exact p = 1.46e-5, recomputed in verify_h1_h2_facts.py). Inspection of results/pilot_family_conservation.tsv and results/pilot_neighbourhoods.tsv shows that K families have higher baseline species counts per family (mean 3.30, median 3.0 primary loci in K vs mean 2.99, median 2.0 in Q\K), longer median host contigs (12,608 bp and 13.0 genes in K vs 10,256.5 bp and 11.0 genes in Q\K), and higher per-locus 4-flank exact-mapping rates (615/1,880 = 32.71% in K vs 765/2,662 = 28.74% in Q\K). Under the Tier 2 3'-stop sensitivity gate (admitting unique same-strand, same-frame 3'-stop matches; n = 637 evaluable families, 43.60% of 1,461), per-locus eligibility equalizes across arms (935/1,880 = 49.73% in K vs 1,316/2,662 = 49.44% in Q\K), family-level eligibility narrows to 45.61% (260/570) in K vs 42.31% (377/891) in Q\K, and the covariate-stratified Mantel-Haenszel contrasts remain non-significant: Delta_MH(E_fam) = +0.018391 (null SD 0.031223, p = 0.561844) and Delta_MH(C_fam) = +0.043859 (null SD 0.034337, p = 0.232277; results/pilot_conservation_summary.json lines 212-220).

By contrast, in Tier 3 (eligible_sensitivity, requiring at least 1 exact-mapped flank per locus across at least 2 species; n = 1,320 evaluable families: 521 K, 799 Q\K), results/pilot_conservation_summary.json (lines 106-125) reports only an unadjusted comparison: 33.5893% (175/521) in K vs 24.6558% (197/799) in Q\K (raw difference +0.089334, odds ratio 1.545583, Fisher exact p = 0.000458; mean R_fam 0.256994 vs 0.190511, Mann-Whitney p = 0.000467). Crucially, scripts/03_analyse_conservation.py does not compute a same-contig positional reference (E_fam) or stratified permutation contrast for Tier 3, and defines ko_annotation_opportunity_partial (line 259) as t3_ko_flanks / t3_callable_flanks, which divides out the number of callable flanks per locus. Because K loci in Tier 3 have more exact-mapped flanks per locus (mean 2.80 vs 2.71 in Q\K) and more partial-window species per family (mean 3.22 vs 2.91 in Q\K), K families accumulate more total flanking KO trials per family in Tier 3. The manuscript and Supplement must therefore report the 75.84% Tier 1 attrition rate alongside the Tier 2 sensitivity results and must not cite the unadjusted Tier 3 p-value (p = 0.000458) as evidence that low-query-TM families (K) have stronger neighborhood conservation than fold-matched families (Q\K).

5. H2 cross-family dependence, coarse phylum pooling, mobilome keyword-screen limits, and missing Bakta annotations.
Four additional data constraints in H2 must be stated in the Supplementary Information:
First, across the 353 Tier 1 evaluable families, the 877 evaluable primary loci reside on 869 unique (bin, contig) pairs (with 8 contigs shared across multiple families and 0 overlapping 4-flank windows), 617 unique refined bins (145 shared across multiple families), and 568 unique dRep species clusters (153 shared across multiple families; results/pilot_conservation_summary.json lines 647-661, verified in verify_h1_h2_facts.py). Thus families and host genomes are not mutually independent observations, and within-stratum label permutation tests conditional exchangeability given the observed strata rather than proving cross-family independence. In the sensitivity subset of 112 Tier 1 families (51 K, 61 Q\K) whose evaluable loci share zero dRep species with any other Tier 1 family, mean excess recurrence E_fam is +0.261855 in K vs +0.198430 in Q\K (unadjusted difference +0.063425; results/pilot_conservation_summary.json lines 662-669).
Second, the stratified permutation and regression models collapse 16 GTDB r232 phyla into four coarse groups: Actinomycetota (n = 76: 43 K, 33 Q\K), Thermoplasmatota (n = 78: 35 K, 43 Q\K), multi (n = 126 cross-phylum families: 56 K, 70 Q\K), and other_bacteria_archaea (n = 73: 39 K, 34 Q\K, pooling 14 phyla including Myxococcota_A n = 19, Acidobacteriota n = 13, Pseudomonadota n = 9, and Planctomycetota n = 7). Cross-phylum families (phylum_group = multi) have substantially lower syntenic recurrence than Thermoplasmatota in multivariable OLS/HC3 (beta = -0.160060 +/- 0.056174, p = 0.004381 on E_fam; beta = -0.198166 +/- 0.060164, p = 0.000989 on raw C_fam; results/pilot_conservation_summary.json lines 335-343 and 417-425).
Third, while 0 of the 128 Tier 1 conserved families matched the 11-keyword mobilome screen on their single top recurrent KO (best_ko_mobilome_flag == 0), this keyword check on one KO per family does not establish that the surrounding genomic neighborhoods are non-mobilome or cellular rather than mobile elements, particularly given that 62.68% (3,460/5,520) of Tier 1 flanks carry no KO assignment (mapped_noKO; RESULTS.md Section 8).
Fourth, while 2,041 of the 2,126 refined bins (96.00%) were verified by exact uppercase contig SHA-256 hashing and Bakta .log orig-id mapping with zero hash or reverse-complement mismatches, 85 refined bins (4.00%, accounting for 108 primary flanks) lack Bakta/eggNOG annotation files on IBEX (classified as source_file_unavailable in results/pilot_join_metrics.json).
