The manuscript titled "Known phyla, unknown proteome: a controlled census of the Rub' al-Khali soil metagenome" presents a metagenomic assembly, binning, functional annotation, and ESMFold structural census of 522 soil and hypolithic samples from the hyper-arid Rub' al-Khali desert, recovering 4,768 quality-filtered metagenome-assembled genomes (1,622 dereplicated species clusters across 32 phyla) and 17.5 million protein clusters. Under the explicit scope override for this Round 7 review, this report evaluates the completed extension packages for Hypothesis 1 (H1, phylogenetic generalized least squares modeling of sequence novelty across desert Thermoplasmatota species representatives, archived in reviews/darkmatter-2026-10-13/round6/snapshots/h1_final and summarized in reviews/darkmatter-2026-10-13/round7/snapshots/h2_initial/H1_COMPLETENESS.md) and Hypothesis 2 (H2, genomic neighborhood synteny and flanking KEGG Orthology conservation across the full 1,461-family multi-species Gate 3 structural dark proteome cohort, archived in reviews/darkmatter-2026-10-13/round7/snapshots/h2_initial). Hypotheses 3 and 4 remain running on IBEX and are deferred to separate reviews.

Both H1 and H2 have reached full execution completeness at their target census scales, with verified Slurm completion records on IBEX (jobs 53704703 and 53738443, exit codes 0:0), intact SHA-256 manifests (67/67 files in h1_final; 26/26 snapshot files and 13/13 result outputs in h2_initial), and exact numerical reproducibility under independent re-execution of the generalised least squares, positional Monte Carlo, Mantel-Haenszel stratified permutation, caliper matching, and heteroskedasticity-consistent regression estimators. Scientifically, both H1 and H2 warrant a verdict of SUPPORTED_WITH_BOUNDS. Neither analysis produces a standalone positive mechanism that transforms the manuscript from a descriptive metagenomic resource into a mechanistic evolutionary paper, and neither should be promoted as a main-text headline figure. However, both provide rigorous, self-correcting negative and conditioning controls that bound the manuscript's central claims: H1 shows that species-level dark-proteome fraction in desert Thermoplasmatota is heavily structured by vertical lineage history (Pagel's lambda = 0.9158) while retaining conditional divergence associations with proteome-wide amino-acid identity and shared gene-content overlap, and H2 shows that while multi-species structural dark families occupy non-random syntenic neighborhoods within both known-fold (K) and novel-fold candidate (Q\K) arms, the apparent between-arm conservation advantage of K disappears once species breadth, taxonomic composition, and flanking annotation density are controlled. One major documentation defect in the newly added Round 7 file H1_COMPLETENESS.md (corrupted OLS and PGLS entries in Section 4) and several methodological and attrition bounds must be addressed before integrating these results into manuscript/main.tex and the Supplementary Information.

3. H1 methodological bounds: finite-population interpretation, conditional suppression of AAI vs RBH, external comparator scope, and dual gene-caller basis.
Four methodological bounds govern how H1 may be reported in the manuscript:
First, the n = 237 genomes constitute the entire finite population of dereplicated Rub' al-Khali Thermoplasmatota species representatives in this study, not a random sample drawn from a larger catalog. Therefore, the Student-t intervals (df = 230) and p-values in S1 through S7 are model-based quantities describing realizations of a hypothetical Brownian-motion evolutionary process on a single fixed FastTree topology (thermo_tree.nwk), conditional on the plug-in estimate lambda_ML = 0.9158. While profile-likelihood propagation across lambda in [0.8463, 0.9578] widens the 95% intervals slightly to [+0.000836, +0.002534] for aai and [-0.176359, -0.095479] for frac_rbh_desert (round6/snapshots/h1_final/DOSSIER.md, Section 4.1), phylogenetic uncertainty across bootstrap tree topologies is not propagated.
Second, the opposite signs of aai (+0.001704) and frac_rbh_desert (-0.137484) in the multivariable PGLS model reflect conditional variance partitioning rather than opposite bivariate marginal trends. Marginally across the 237 species, both aai (Pearson r = -0.088) and frac_rbh_desert (r = -0.351) are negatively correlated with f_novel, and aai and frac_rbh_desert are positively correlated with one another (r = +0.493; OLS VIFs 1.70 and 1.54). Holding orthologous gene-content overlap (frac_rbh_desert) and assembly size (log_len) fixed, higher amino-acid identity in the conserved core is associated with a higher dark fraction in the remaining accessory proteome. The manuscript must frame both coefficients strictly as partial regression slopes conditional on one another and on genome size/fragmentation, and must not claim a positive marginal correlation between AAI and novelty or invoke horizontal gene transfer or de novo gene birth as demonstrated mechanisms.
Third, the 237 nearest non-desert comparators resolve to only 32 unique external reference genomes (229 species matched to 25 GTDB r232 species representatives and 8 species matched to 7 MGnify environmental MAGs), all of which are uncultured metagenome-assembled genomes rather than cultivated type strains. One species representative (bin_id = 35174, the sole representative of class c__Thermoplasmata) has no within-class external reference in the comparator pool and is matched cross-class to a c__Poseidoniia genome at 48.68% AAI (excluded in sensitivity S6, n = 236, where beta_AAI = +0.001711, p = 3.87e-5 and beta_RBH = -0.137574, p = 1.94e-11).
Fourth, f_novel uses the catalogue protein count (n_proteins_cat from metagenomic Pyrodigal) as its denominator, whereas frac_rbh_desert uses the CheckM2 single-genome Pyrodigal protein call set (n_prot_aai; median ratio n_prot_aai / n_proteins_cat = 1.0020, range 0.8966 to 1.0804), and 5 of the 237 species representatives (48PRr1__SemiBin_020, 062, 095, 111, and 112) lack archived CheckM2 .faa files on disk and are excluded in the harmonized-denominator sensitivity S7 (n = 232, where beta_AAI = +0.001432, p = 6.95e-4 and beta_RBH = -0.145859, p = 7.72e-11).

4. H2 complete-window attrition (75.84% loss in Tier 1), differential survival between K and Q\K, and confounding in the Tier 3 partial-window sensitivity gate.
In H2 (reviews/darkmatter-2026-10-13/round7/snapshots/h2_initial), moving from the 2,838 confidence-qualified structural dark families (pLDDT >= 0.70, monomer) to the Gate 3 multi-species cohort (>= 2 dRep 95% ANI species clusters) retains 1,461 families (570 K, 891 Q\K). However, enforcing the primary Tier 1 complete-window gate (requiring >= 2 distinct species whose primary representative locus has all 4 flanking genes m2, m1, p1, p2 present on the contig and mapped by exact start/stop/strand coordinates to Bakta/eggNOG) eliminates 1,108 of the 1,461 families (75.84% attrition), leaving only 353 evaluable families (24.16%). Furthermore, Tier 1 survival is strongly differential by arm: 173 / 570 (30.35%) of K families are evaluable versus 180 / 891 (20.20%) of Q\K families (two-sided Fisher exact p = 1.15e-5). Inspection of results/pilot_family_conservation.tsv and results/pilot_neighbourhoods.tsv shows that this 10.15 percentage-point survival gap arises because K families have wider baseline species breadth (mean 3.30, median 3.0 primary species loci per family in K vs mean 2.99, median 2.0 in Q\K), longer median contigs (12,608 bp and 13.0 genes in K vs 10,256.5 bp and 11.0 genes in Q\K), and slightly fewer 5' start-codon shifts relative to Bakta (11.2% stop3prime-matched flanks in K vs 12.7% in Q\K). When 5' start-codon differences are admitted in Tier 2 (eligible_stop3_sens, n = 637 families, 43.60% of 1,461), per-locus eligibility equalizes across arms (49.73% in K vs 49.44% in Q\K), family-level eligibility converges to 45.61% (260/570) in K vs 42.31% (377/891) in Q\K, and the covariate-stratified Mantel-Haenszel contrast between arms shrinks further toward zero (Delta_MH(E_fam) = +0.0184, p = 0.5618; Delta_MH(C_fam) = +0.0258, p = 0.5067).

By contrast, for Tier 3 (eligible_sensitivity, requiring >= 1 exact-mapped flank per locus across >= 2 species; n = 1,320 evaluable families: 521 K, 799 Q\K), results/pilot_conservation_summary.json reports only an unadjusted raw comparison: 33.59% (175/521) in K vs 24.66% (197/799) in Q\K (raw difference +8.93 percentage points, Fisher p = 0.000458; or 30.70% vs 22.11% if expressed over all 1,461 families). Crucially, scripts/03_analyse_conservation.py does not compute a same-contig positional null (E_fam) or stratified Mantel-Haenszel contrast for Tier 3, and defines ko_annotation_opportunity_partial (line 259) as t3_ko_flanks / t3_callable_flanks, which divides out the number of callable flanks per locus. Because K loci in Tier 3 have both more exact-mapped flanks per locus (mean 2.80 vs 2.71 in Q\K) and more partial-window species per family (mean 3.22 vs 2.91 in Q\K), K families have more total flanking KO trials per family in Tier 3, mechanically inflating the unadjusted recurrence rate. Consequently, the manuscript and supplement must not cite the unadjusted Tier 3 raw p-value (p = 0.000458) as evidence that K is more syntenically conserved than Q\K; Tier 3 must be explicitly labeled as an unadjusted partial-window sensitivity that is confounded by unequal flank completeness and species breadth.

5. H2 cross-family species/bin sharing, coarse phylum pooling, and missing Bakta source files.
Three structural features of the H2 dataset further bound its statistical interpretation:
First, the 353 Tier 1 evaluable families (877 primary loci) are not independent observations: they draw from 568 unique dRep species clusters, 617 refined bins, and 846 unique (bin, contig) pairs, with 153 species clusters (26.9%), 145 bins (23.5%), and 30 contigs shared across multiple families (0 overlapping 4-flank windows). Restricting to the 76 Tier 1 families (31 K, 45 Q\K) whose eligible loci share zero dRep species with any other Tier 1 family yields a slightly negative point difference (mean E_fam = +0.2896 in K vs +0.3052 in Q\K, difference = -0.0156; results/pilot_conservation_summary.json).
Second, the stratified permutation and multivariable regression models pool 16 distinct GTDB r232 phyla into four coarse categories: Actinomycetota (n = 76), Thermoplasmatota (n = 78), multi (n = 126 cross-phylum MMseqs2 families), and other_bacteria_archaea (n = 73, which combines 14 bacterial and archaeal phyla including Myxococcota_A n = 19, Acidobacteriota n = 13, Pseudomonadota n = 9, and Planctomycetota n = 7). Cross-phylum families (phylum_group = multi) exhibit markedly lower syntenic recurrence (OLS beta = -0.1597 +/- 0.0556, p = 0.0044 on E_fam; beta = -0.3125 +/- 0.0623, p = 8.4e-7 on C_fam) and represent a higher share of Q\K (70/180 = 38.9%) than K (56/173 = 32.4%).
Third, while 2,041 of the 2,126 unique bins (96.0%) were verified by exact uppercase contig sequence SHA-256 hashing and Bakta .log orig-id mapping with zero hash or orientation mismatches, 85 refined bins (4.0%, accounting for 27 primary loci and 108 primary flanks) lack Bakta/eggNOG output directories on IBEX (classified as source_file_unavailable in results/pilot_join_metrics.json). These bounds should be stated concisely in the Supplementary Methods.
