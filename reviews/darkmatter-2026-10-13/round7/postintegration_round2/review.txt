The manuscript titled "Known phyla, unknown proteome: a controlled census of the Rub' al-Khali soil metagenome" presents a metagenomic and structural census of microbial dark matter across 551 soil samples collected from 59 core transect sites and two auxiliary locations along a 1,043 km transect of the Rub' al-Khali desert over five expeditions spanning 31 months. From 549 assembled samples, two SemiBin2 binnings reconciled with DAS_Tool yield 14,426 refined bins and 5,438 dereplicated species-level metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs; 126 MIMAG high-quality and 5,312 medium-quality), all placed to class rank within 51 GTDB release 232 phyla (with first empty taxonomic rank at order for 11 representatives, family for 142, genus for 1,343, species for 3,755, and 187 carrying a species assignment). Gene prediction across the 552 assembly files produces 753,305,533 proteins collapsed into 449,265,306 90%-identity cluster representatives; sequence darkness is defined by the absence of any UniRef50 (release 2026_01) hit under an E-value threshold with no identity or coverage filter, identifying 69,938,628 dark representatives (15.6%) assembly-wide and 4,292,446 of 43,423,322 genome-resolved representatives (9.89%), from which 106,042 recurrent dark families (536,437 member genes) are admitted across at least three samples and two sites. The authors evaluate the coding status, evolutionary constraint, genomic context, reference-database recovery, and structural coverage of this repertoire against controls at each layer: +1 shifted-frame and divergence-stratified UniRef50-matched families for pairwise dN/dS, 722 long-read genomes of cultured desert isolates across 22 shared species pairs, equal-depth DIAMOND searches against soil (SMAG) versus global (GEM) reference catalogues, within-phylum phylogenetic generalised least squares (PGLS) across 237 Thermoplasmatota species representatives, flanking KEGG orthologue recurrence across multi-species dark structural families, and paired whole-chain versus confidence-cropped ESMFold and Foldseek searches against PDB alongside 3,013 blinded known-fold controls. Across these comparisons, recurrent dark families show reading-frame-dependent purifying selection, soil-derived references recover nearly three times as many dark queries as length-matched global references (28.35% versus 9.81%), within-Thermoplasmatota dark fractions associate positively with nearest-comparator amino-acid identity (AAI) and negatively with reciprocal-best-hit (RBH) gene-content coverage, flanking KEGG orthologues recur above same-contig positional baselines within both structural dark groups (31.75 and 25.67 percentage points) with an inconclusive adjusted difference between groups (+3.54 percentage points, p = 0.41), and confidence-guided cropping recovers PDB structural matches (query-normalised TM-score >= 0.5) for 74.07% (714 of 964) of initially low-query-score dark models that remained unmatched as whole chains.

This second and final post-integration review assesses whether the revised manuscript and updated verification package close the valid criticisms from Round 1 and evaluates readiness for Nature Communications. All seven substantive and editorial issues raised in Round 1 have been resolved in the revised manuscript and its companion verification suite. Specifically, Supplementary Notes S1 to S3 are now ordered to match their first citations in the main text; Supplementary Note S1 adds the complete whole-chain-to-crop transition accounting for the 1,031 initially low-query-score dark models, the disaggregated whole-chain and crop match counts for the 1,807 other dark models (1,107 initial PDB matches and 700 initial AlphaFold-only matches), the CPU and GPU control crop counts across all sequence-identity exclusion tiers, and the CPU row-exclusion audit for out-of-range alignment-normalised scores; the Figure 4 caption explains why whole-chain classifications can differ between the initial dual-reference screen and the deeper paired PDB search; Methods and Supplementary Note S1 specify the deterministic within-species locus-selection hierarchy, the 16 Mantel-Haenszel strata and n_K * n_Q / (n_K + n_Q) weights, the inclusion of the focal position in the same-contig positional null when eligible, and the exact random seeds (20261013, 20261014, and 20261015); Supplementary Note S1 reconciles the 25,067 pre-matching and 22,310 post-matching shared sequences between SMAG and GEM; Methods and Supplementary Note S1 specify the FastTree phylogeny and the exclusion of the single Thermoplasmata representative in the same-class PGLS sensitivity; and all three local verification scripts pass with zero failures. My verdict for the four newly integrated analyses and their manuscript integration is ACCEPT_WITH_BOUNDS. Regarding separate Nature Communications submission readiness, while the newly integrated analyses and compact verification driver are scientifically sound and locally reproducible, whole-paper submission readiness remains contingent on resolving the substantive provenance, claim-migration, and data-deposition prerequisites explicitly documented in Data and code availability (78 missing raw-file paths, 302 permission failures, unverified duplicate-run merge commands, incomplete historical claim-registry migration, pending MIMAG MAG deposition and archived release DOI, and confirmation of public or reviewer access to ENA study PRJEB125153).

2. Soundness of statistical conditioning and interpretive bounds in the revised manuscript. The revised text maintains disciplined interpretive boundaries across all four newly integrated sections:
   a. Structural crop recovery as a joint intervention: Because the packaged Foldseek build omits one residue from inclusive alignment spans when computing alignment-normalised TM-scores, the manuscript excludes alignment-normalised threshold subdivisions from biological inference and defines all structural cohorts and endpoints strictly via query-normalised TM-score (qTM >= 0.5). Pooling the 1,031 dark models with initial dual-reference qTM < 0.5 and showing that 714 of 964 whole-chain-unmatched models recover a PDB match upon confidence cropping (731 of 961 on GPU) is properly framed as a joint operational intervention: as the text notes, "Cropping jointly changes sequence extent, score normalisation and search opportunity." The design therefore does not isolate unconfident-tail removal from length-dependent score normalisation or retrieval depth as a single mechanism, nor does it claim that the remaining unmatched crops represent validated novel folds.
   b. Genomic-context recurrence and window ascertainment: The manuscript transparently reports the attrition from 2,838 confidence-qualified dark families to 1,461 multi-species families and 353 primary complete-window families (30.35% retention in the low-query-score group versus 20.20% in the reference-matched group), treats the between-group adjusted contrast (+3.54 percentage points, p = 0.41; -0.54 points across 96 strict pairs, p = 0.92) as inconclusive, and bounds the within-group excess recurrence (31.75 and 25.67 percentage points above same-contig positional background, p = 0.0002) to callable multi-species families.
   c. Biome-matched versus global reference recovery: The manuscript clearly states that the matched SMAG versus GEM experiment compares one fixed 1,500-genome SMAG sample against three 2,055-genome GEM draws across a finite census of 106,042 dark and 106,042 control queries. It properly interprets the 18.54 percentage-point dark-query recovery advantage (16.83 to 18.62 points across sensitivities; positive in all 40 dark length-by-run strata) as a finite-catalogue reference-coverage comparison rather than biological replication or a causal test of soil adaptation.
   d. Phylogeny-conditioned associations within Thermoplasmatota: The PGLS model across all 237 Thermoplasmatota representatives properly conditions on shared branch lengths (Pagel's lambda = 0.9158, 95% profile-likelihood interval 0.8463-0.9578; whitened R^2 = 0.4691), reports that the 237 genome-comparator pairs reuse 32 distinct external reference accessions (229 pairs using GTDB R232 representatives and 8 using MGnify genomes; 142 soil, 3 marine sediment, and 92 with unknown environmental metadata), and frames the positive AAI slope (0.001704, p = 4.07e-5) and negative RBH coverage slope (-0.137484, p = 1.88e-11) as conditional cross-sectional associations rather than reconstructed gene-gain or gene-loss histories.

3. Distinction between support for the revised H1-H4 sections and substantive whole-paper prerequisites for Nature Communications submission. It is essential to separate the completed empirical and computational support for the newly integrated sections from the remaining whole-paper provenance and deposition gaps candidly disclosed in the manuscript:
   a. External data access and archival depositions: In Data and code availability, the manuscript reports that private ENA submission receipts map 554 sequencing runs from the 551 soil samples to study PRJEB125153 with a recorded release date of 1 November 2026 ("the public filereport returned zero runs on 13 October 2026. The recorded release date is 1 November 2026; public release or reviewer access remains to be confirmed before submission").    b. Upstream raw-to-final provenance and environmental metadata gaps: While the compact reproducibility driver reproduces the active table calculations and figures from preserved summary inputs, Data and code availability explicitly states that "complete raw-to-final reproduction is limited by 78 missing raw-file paths, 302 permission failures, unverified duplicate-run merge commands and incomplete historical claim-registry migration.
