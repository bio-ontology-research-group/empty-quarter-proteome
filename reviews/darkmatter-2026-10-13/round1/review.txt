P4 DARK PROTEOME — ROUND 1 INDEPENDENT REFEREE REPORT
Manuscript: manuscript/main.tex (identical to paper/main.tex), "Known phyla, unknown proteome: a controlled census of the Rub' al-Khali soil metagenome"
Reviewer role: independent critical scientific reviewer, round 1 of 3. Review only: no manuscript, code or data was edited and no job was submitted.

======================================================================
SUMMARY
======================================================================

The manuscript describes a genome-resolved catalogue from a hyperarid sand sea: 551 samples, 5 expeditions, 5,438 species representatives. It makes five claims about the sequence-dark fraction:
(i) 9.89% of genome-resolved clusters are dark, and these clusters behave as coding sequence (recurrence, persistence, dN/dS against a matched control and a +1-frame null, reappearance in cultured genomes);
(ii) dark proteins are strain-variable accessory content (80% vs 16% reciprocal sharing between cultured and MAG genomes of the same species);
(iii) lineage, not genome quality, sets darkness, and a Thermoplasmatota radiation is 4.5 to 6.5 times darker than matched genomes;
(iv) structural novelty is "architectural, not local" against a blinded known-fold floor;
(v) biome-matched references explain more of the dark proteome than generic depth.

The design is unusually well controlled for this literature. Many disclosures are honest: the frame-null caveat, the TM normalisation dependence, and the control asymmetries. Most headline numbers reproduce exactly from the snapshot tables.

The problems are in scope and inference. Several headline claims are stated more broadly than the analyses license:
- The coding tests apply to 12.5% of dark representatives, not to "the unknown tenth".
- The length and partiality statistics quoted for the genome-resolved census are assembly-wide values.
- The cultured-genome result that carries the abstract and Discussion comes from an analysis its own README marks "EXPLORATORY, NOT REGISTERED". One of its 23 pairs is the E. coli genome that the authors' own no-template control identifies as a reagent contaminant.
- The dN/dS control is not matched on divergence. At matched divergence the dark families are markedly less constrained.
- The structural floor uses PDB-derived controls searched against references that contain them, with no self-hit exclusion. It does not calibrate the near-threshold failure mode the candidates show.
- "Architectural not local" is not demonstrated; the Pfam data point the other way.

None of these is fatal to a solid paper. Most can be fixed with re-scoping and a small set of targeted analyses that use data already on IBEX. Figures 2 and 3 are placeholders and Figure 1 is the v1 illustration; I treat them as incomplete, not as evidence.

Evidence levels used below:
- [SOURCE]: code or README inspected.
- [TABLE]: I recomputed the value from a snapshot table.
- [CONFIRMED]: a manuscript statement contradicts the code or data.
- [PLAUSIBLE]: needs a test.

R1-01 — CRITICAL — CONFIRMED. The "coding" evidence covers 12.5% of the dark set, but the abstract and Discussion generalise it to all dark clusters.

Manuscript text:
- Abstract: "A tenth of their protein clusters (9.89%) have no reference homolog. These form 106,042 families, 79.4% recurring in all five expeditions, under purifying selection."
- Discussion: "The unknown tenth of this soil proteome is the accessory genome ... The same 106,042 families return in every expedition ... so they are biology."

Evidence:
- darkfam_basis_clean/results/family_size_hist.txt: of the 4,292,446 genome-resolved dark representatives, 3,353,521 are singletons and 196,450 are in two-member clusters at 50%/80%. Only 108,826 clusters have three or more members.
- After the sample/site gate (fam_2_recurrence.sh lines 92–98), the admitted families hold 536,437 members, 12.5% of dark representatives.
- The manuscript names only "the 782,616 clusters with one or two variants and the 2,784 within-genome paralog sets". It omits roughly 2.77 million non-recurrent singletons and doubletons that were never tested.

Two further scope problems:
- "Their protein clusters" implies the 5,438 genomes. The 9.89% denominator is clusters with any member on any of the 37,949 unrefined HQ/MQ bins of both binnings (darkclean_5_mag.sh). The per-representative instance rate is 7.85% (1,600,067/20,379,930; lineage v2_scan_report). SOURCE_AUDIT.md flags the same conflation in a Flash summary; the abstract repeats it.
- Persistence is measured on families admitted for being recurrent (≥3 samples, ≥2 sites), so it is partly conditioned on its own outcome.

Fix: re-scope the abstract, results and Discussion to "the recurrent dark families". Add the full admission funnel to Fig 2a, at representative, protein-instance and family level. Report what share of dark protein instances, and of per-genome darkness, the admitted families represent. For the non-admitted 87.5%, either test a subset (dN/dS is impossible for singletons, but shadow-ORF and coding-potential screens are not; see R1-03) or state that it is untested.

R1-02 — CRITICAL — CONFIRMED. The length and partiality figures come from a different scope from the census they are attached to.

Manuscript text:
- "Restricting to clusters ... gives the genome-resolved census ... Length, not assembly completeness, separates dark from annotated clusters: 76% of dark representatives are shorter than 100 residues against 30% of all representatives, while partial gene calls are equally common in both (86.7% and 87.5%)."
- Also: "(the 76% of dark representatives under 100 residues are outside this test)".

Evidence:
- darkmatter_clean/06_dark_lengths/SUMMARY.txt and all_reps_length_summary.txt give the assembly-wide figures: 52,967,341/69,938,628 = 75.7% under 100 aa; 29.7% of all 449M representatives; 60,612,661/69,938,628 = 86.7% partial; 393,114,691/449,265,306 = 87.5% partial.
- The genome-resolved dark set (mag_dark_length_summary.txt; cultured step3_length_summary; current_table_checks.json) is 65.83% under 100 aa and 56.40% incomplete.
- The genome-resolved denominator of all 43.4M representatives was never profiled, so "equally common in both" is untested at that scope.
- The comparator is "all representatives", which includes the dark ones, not annotated representatives.
- The structural screen covers 1,466,943/4,292,446 = 34.2% of the genome-resolved set, so the excluded share is 65.8%, not 76%.

Fix: run the darkclean_7 profile on the 43.4M genome-resolved representatives, split dark vs non-dark. Report dark rate by length × partial stratum (complete calls ≥100 aa at minimum). Correct both sentences.

R1-03 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (logic) / PLAUSIBLE (magnitude). Three of the four "coding" tests test the DNA, not the reading frame. The frame null does not exclude shadow ORFs.

Manuscript text: "Four tests separate encoded biology from spurious open reading frames", and in the Discussion: "spurious open reading frames do not recur in most samples, persist across five expeditions, sit under purifying selection ... or reappear in closed cultured genomes."

Problems:
- A spurious ORF called on a real, persistent genome recurs, persists and reappears wherever that genome does. Recurrence, persistence and cultured reappearance establish that the DNA is real, not that it is coding. Only the dN/dS legs bear on coding status.
- A +1-shift null distinguishes the annotated frame from one alternative frame only. An antisense shadow ORF lying in the phase where its third positions coincide with the third positions of a real gene on the other strand inherits low dN/dS by projection. This is the overlapping-gene problem analysed by Nelson et al. 2020 (OLGenie; full text verified). Long antisense ORFs are common in high-GC genomes, and Actinomycetota carry 32.8% of attributable dark families.
- 7.6% of evaluable families have shifted-frame dN/dS at or below the annotated frame [TABLE: 1 − 0.9239 over 9,029 pairs]. 37.7% of families have null dN/dS below 1.

Smallest decisive analysis:
(a) Search the other five frames, at least the reverse complement, of admitted-family member CDS against UniRef50. Any hit marks a shadow of a known gene.
(b) Run AntiFam on the admitted families.
(c) Check opposite-strand overlap with non-dark gene calls on the same contigs.
(d) Report a family-level frame-specificity flag (real < null) and carry it into downstream statistics.
(e) Remove "spurious ORFs do not recur/persist" from the Discussion.

R1-04 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED by recomputation. The dN/dS "matched control" is not matched on divergence. At matched divergence, dark families are much less constrained.

Manuscript text: "under purifying selection of the same kind and order as known genes measured on the same instrument", and Methods: "Both push the control toward more reference absence and lower dN/dS, so both comparisons ... are conservative."

Evidence:
- fam_5_control.sh lines 102–148 draw a length-stratified random sample of about 4.29M from roughly 39M non-dark genome-resolved representatives, then cluster that subsample. The dark arm clusters all dark representatives. Subsampling removes close relatives, so control families are more divergent.
- [TABLE, dnds.tsv]: median family mean_dS is 0.607 dark vs 0.863 control; median mean_pid is 0.735 vs 0.674.
- Stratified medians of the family median dN/dS:
  dS ≤ 0.10: dark 0.582 (n=9,201) vs control 0.321 (n=690)
  dS 0.10–0.25: 0.540 vs 0.332
  dS 0.25–0.50: 0.496 vs 0.463
  dS 0.50–1.0: 0.435 vs 0.452
  dS 1–2: 0.254 vs 0.293
  pid > 0.95: 0.428 vs 0.162 (7,213 vs 774 families)
  pid 0.90–0.95: 0.528 vs 0.234
- The pooled 0.425 vs 0.388 therefore reflects a composition difference. Among closely related sequences, dark families sit at roughly 1.7 to 2.6 times the control's dN/dS. That fits a mixture of weakly constrained or non-coding members, or of recent accessory genes (the time-dependence of dN/dS).
- The "conservative" claim is asserted, not shown. The missing sample/site gate on the control retains within-sample sets of unknown dN/dS behaviour.

Fix:
- Rebuild the control from the full non-dark pool, with identical sample/site admission.
- Match or stratify on dS (or pid), phylum and GC.
- Report stratified medians in the main text and drop the "conservative" sentence unless it is demonstrated.

R1-05 — MODERATE — CONFIRMED. The frame-null denominators are unstated, and the mechanism needs disclosure.

Manuscript text: "on a seeded subset of 10,000 dark families ... give a median pairwise dN/dS of 1.087 (37.7% below one) ... 0.427 (97.0% below one), and the shifted frame is the higher of the two in 92.4% of families."

Evidence:
- The subset was drawn from families with ≥3 CDS in the index (fam_6c_dnds.sh lines 47–56), not from the 97,200 evaluable families.
- Outputs: 9,148 null, 9,182 annotated, 9,029 paired (current_table_checks.json; reproduced). Methods says "10,000 evaluable dark families".
- [TABLE] Paired, null dS is 0.53 times the annotated-frame dS and null dN is 1.34 times. The value above 1 is largely a mechanical rotation of the codon-position substitution pattern; the README says so, the manuscript does not.
- Only 94.2% of subset families reproduce the shipped full-arm value exactly. In the masked true-frame arm, members with internal stops are retained instead of dropped. That means some annotated-frame CDS contain internal stops, which should not happen for Pyrodigal calls. This points to excision/phase problems for some members (slice_cds.py takes coordinates verbatim, including for partial genes).

Fix:
- State n for each statistic.
- Report the fraction of member CDS with internal stops in the annotated frame and why.
- Soften "A set dominated by spurious calls would not behave this way" to the specific alternative excluded.

R1-06 — CRITICAL — CONFIRMED. The cultured-vs-MAG accessory claim rests on an unregistered exploratory analysis that includes a known contaminant, and alternative explanations are untested.

Manuscript text:
- Abstract: "cultured and metagenome genomes share 80% of their annotated proteins but 16% of their dark ones: dark proteins are strain-variable content".
- Discussion: "... reappear in closed cultured genomes."

Evidence:
- cultured_comparison_clean/README.md, section 3.6: "EXPLORATORY, NOT REGISTERED ... nothing in the paper rests on them." The paper's abstract rests on them.
- The method is RBH at ≥50% identity and ≥50% coverage of both proteins, with -k 5. The Methods instead describe a -k 500 reciprocal-coverage search for a different analysis. The pair anatomy is not described in Methods at all.
- One of the 23 pairs is V42PRr2__single_SemiBin_33, Escherichia coli (step3_paired_by_species.tsv; mag_meta.tsv). contamination_clean/README.md line 59 identifies exactly this representative as the reagent contaminant: half of the assigned no-template-control reads, present in 316/550 samples, anti-correlated with input DNA. Pairs also include Acinetobacter baumannii, Enterococcus_B lactis and the Bacillus_A anthracis group (README caveat). The phrase "species also cultured from these soils" is therefore not safe.
- Statistical weight is small: 1,513 dark MAG proteins pooled over 23 pairs (6 to 162 per pair). "Unchanged on complete calls ≥100 residues" rests on n = 166 dark proteins.
- The gene callers differ. MAGs use Pyrodigal -p meta; Bakta uses Pyrodigal in single mode plus sORF and spurious-ORF filtering. A dark MAG ORF without a protein partner may simply not have been called in the culture, even though its DNA is present.
- The MAG-unique remainder is 45.4% partial and 50.8% contig-edge, which also fits fragmentation or misbinning.
- "Closed cultured genomes" is wrong: the median is 2 contigs and 49% are single-contig (README caveat).

Smallest decisive analyses, all on existing data:
(a) Drop E. coli and other contaminant or human-associated pairs; report n and CI.
(b) Nucleotide-level test: align MAG dark CDS and their contigs to the paired cultured assemblies (minimap2/blastn) to separate "DNA absent" from "not called".
(c) Re-call cultured genomes with the identical Pyrodigal -p meta and repeat.
(d) A direct, MAG-free test of "strain-variable accessory content": five species have 9 to 49 cultured strains. Within each culture-only pangenome, classify dark vs annotated proteins as core or accessory.
(e) Register the analysis and describe it in Methods.
Until (b) to (d) are done, the abstract sentence should be conditional.

R1-07 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED. "Named" means two different things in the paper, which creates internal contradictions.

Manuscript text: abstract "all in named phyla"; Results "Every representative was placed to class rank ... The first rank without a named taxon was order for 11"; lineage section "the median dark fraction falls from 0.217 for genomes unnamed at phylum".

R1-08 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (framing) / PLAUSIBLE (mechanism). The Thermoplasmatota contrast is matched on the wrong axis, and its mechanism is untested even though the data exist.

Manuscript text:
- Abstract: "237 species in two Thermoplasmatota classes are four to six times darker than matched genomes and carry a fifth of the dark families."
- Results: "Matching enlarges the contrast, because the unmatched comparators of similar novelty were themselves unusually dark."

Evidence:
- The matched null (v2_matched_null.py; summary) matches on quality, abundance and prevalence, not on novelty or reference proximity, so the "similar novelty" explanation does not follow from the design.
- At matched first-unnamed rank (class), Thermoplasmatota are 0.310 vs 0.125 for 324 other class-level genomes (README; [TABLE] reproduced). That is about 2.5×. Other class-novel phyla sit at 0.21 to 0.25 (Planctomycetota 0.210, Patescibacteriota 0.222, Bdellovibrionota 0.248).
- The 237 pairs are one clade, so "darker in 237 of 237" is not 237 independent observations.
- "Two classes": one representative is Thermoplasmata, so there are three classes.
- "A fifth of the dark families" is 19.84% of 77,069 single-phylum attributable families. Attributable families are 74% of the 106,042, so the share of all families is about 14.6%.

Positive check: protein density does not suggest gene fragmentation from a recoded stop codon. Thermoplasmatota have 1,070 proteins/Mb vs 1,079 for Halobacteriota and 1,008 for Actinomycetota [TABLE].

Decisive analyses on existing data:
(a) Search the 237 genomes' dark proteins against the already Pyrodigal-called 218 GTDB R232 and 29 distinct MGnify genomes of these classes (v2_thermo_relatives, unregistered). This directly measures "dark = absent from UniProt but present in known relatives".
(b) Compute the UniRef50 dark fraction of those GTDB genomes themselves. If they are also about 30% dark, darkness is a class/reference property, not a desert property.
(c) Report the novelty-matched contrast in the abstract alongside, or instead of, 4.5 to 6.5×.
(d) Optionally run Codetta on a subset to close the genetic-code question cheaply.

R1-09 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (code) / PLAUSIBLE (size of bias). The structural "blinded floor" is not matched in difficulty to the candidates.

Manuscript text: "Standardised to the candidates' confidence distribution, the candidate rate is 6.6 times the floor ...", and "calibrated against a 3.35% error rate on known folds".

Evidence and problems:
(a) No homolog exclusion. Controls are PDB chains and crops. 05_integrate.py best_scores takes the maximum over all hits against PDB 250101 and AFDB v6, which contain the source chain or near-identical UniProt entries. The floor therefore measures failure to retrieve one's own or a near-identical structure; the candidates by construction have no sequence homolog. ESMFold was also trained on PDB, so control models are likely memorised. Standardising on pLDDT does not fix either problem.
(b) Selection asymmetry. Candidates were pre-selected for failing the 3Di triage against the same references; controls were not.
(c) The failure modes differ. [TABLE, per_rep/per_control_structures.tsv]: 66 of the 101 control failures return no row at all; only 22 of the 1,031 candidates do. Candidate failures are scored rows near threshold: maximum qTM median 0.392, and 487 of 1,031 (47%) lie between 0.40 and 0.50. The headline count is highly threshold-sensitive (roughly 780 at 0.45, roughly 545 at 0.40), and the floor does not calibrate that regime.
(d) The validation search used -e 10 with no alignment-length gate (05c_afdb.sh), so "96.7% contain a region that aligns to a known structure" is ungated.

CLAUDE.md requires retrieval decomposition; the current text does not report it. The superseded version did ("74 have no row ... 534 have a retrieved row").

Fix:
- Re-score controls excluding hits at ≥30% sequence identity, or the same CATH superfamily/Pfam clan (a held-out remote-homology floor).
- Pass controls through the same 3Di triage and condition on triage failure.
- Report K and the floor at qTM 0.40, 0.45 and 0.50, plus the no-row/below-threshold decomposition.
- Require minimum aligned length and E-value for the alnTM statement.

R1-10 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (overinterpretation). "Novelty is architectural, not local" is not demonstrated, and the Pfam data argue against it.

Manuscript text: "what remains unmatched is the whole-protein arrangement rather than its parts"; section title "Structural novelty is architectural, not local".

Evidence (pfam_candidates_clean/results/set_summary.tsv):
- Only 8.75% of K members carry any Pfam domain.
- Where a domain exists it covers a median 25% and leaves a median 159-residue uncovered stretch. That is enough for a domain that has no model, which is local novelty.
- [TABLE] 65.5% of K have target-normalised TM ≥0.5. The query contains a whole known fold plus unexplained sequence, which can be a known domain with a novel domain, a known domain with a disordered or low-confidence extension, or a genuine new combination.
- The 90.7% Pfam rate in controls is circular: PDB chains are Pfam-rich, and candidates are UniRef50-dark by construction. Q-not-K models (structurally covered) also have only 5.6% Pfam, so Pfam does not discriminate here.
- SOURCE_AUDIT.md flags the same overreach in the Flash synthesis; the manuscript shares it.

Fix: segment the 2,838 confident models into domains (Chainsaw or Merizo, both in the verified literature set) and search each domain separately. Classify the 1,031 as (i) all domains known (architectural), (ii) at least one confident domain with no match (local candidate), or (iii) unmatched region of low pLDDT or disorder. Retitle according to the result.

R1-11 — MAJOR — PLAUSIBLE. Persistence by read mapping is permissive and has no null model.

Manuscript text: "79.4% of families are detected in all five expeditions (94.8% in at least four)".

Evidence:
- fam_4_coverm_array.sh runs coverm contig against only the 536,437 member CDS (-p minimap2-sr -m tpm covered_fraction), with no identity, aligned-fraction or minimum-read filter set in the command. Presence is any member with covered fraction ≥0.5, about one to two reads for a 200-nt gene. Reads from homologs outside the index have nowhere else to go.
- The README audit already shows this rule reverses the length–occupancy relation.
- There is no null. Trip 2 has 37 samples and the others 85 to 210 (lineage ecology README). A family present in the median 98 of 551 samples, placed at random, would almost always hit all five expeditions. "All five" therefore largely restates prevalence.

Fix:
- Competitive mapping against all genome-resolved CDS or contigs, with ≥95% identity and ≥2 reads or an aligned-fraction gate.
- A permutation null shuffling expedition labels (within site), preserving family prevalence.
- Sequencing batch as covariate (R1-12).

R1-13 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (overclaim) / PLAUSIBLE (confound). The rarefaction does not isolate "biome-matched" content.

Manuscript text: "a rarefaction model that reproduces the observed MGnify90 absence to 0.15 percentage points"; Discussion "biome-matched references explain 24 points more ... Per sequence added, soil references grow faster in coverage by sampling under-sampled biomes".

Evidence (specificity_clean/README.md):
- At p = 1 the expectation is computed from the same MGnify90 search, so the 0.15-point agreement is a consistency check, not validation of the p < 1 extrapolation.
- The non-desert, genome-level SPIRE (all biomes) gains +21.30 points and SMAG (soil MAGs, unregistered) +27.69, close to the desert union's +24.33. The advantage may be soil/assembled-genome content, or MGnify90 dilution by non-soil clusters, rather than "biome-matched (desert)".
- The desert catalogues are raw, unclustered per-study gene calls (redundant, fragment-rich); MGnify90 is 90%-clustered.
- The control's large negative Atacama gap (−29.97) shows the random-slice assumption fails for narrow catalogues.
- Occupancy of absent families uses the biased presence matrix of R1-11.

Fix:
- Validate the model with two or three real random subsamples of MGnify90.
- Compare against a size-matched non-desert soil catalogue (e.g. a soil-biome MGnify subset or SMAG minus arid MAGs) to isolate "desert" from "soil".
- Decide on SMAG registration.
- Restate the claim as "catalogues assembled from soils/genomes explain more than a random MGnify90 slice" unless the desert-specific comparison holds.

R1-14 — MAJOR — CONFIRMED (data handling) / PLAUSIBLE (inflation). The environmental statistics have censoring, pseudo-replication and compositional problems, and climate provenance is missing.

Evidence:
- results/darkmatter_ecology/env_matrix.tsv [TABLE]: XRF non-detects are recorded as 0.0 in the 251-sample cohort (Mn 197/251, V 88, S 29, Cl 32). eco_3_community.py z-scores them as measured values.
- "Vanadium, molybdenum, nickel, cobalt, manganese and zinc associate with no family" is largely a statement about censored, low-information axes, not biology.
- The vanadium "negative control" is 35% censored and is itself a member of the 11-axis block. It shows that a weak axis is null, not that the design cannot manufacture signal.
- Family-level Spearman/BH tests (54,227 sulfur associations) treat samples as independent despite three replicates per position per visit and repeated visits.
- TPM is compositional. A salt-associated lineage bloom depresses every other family, which by itself could produce "90 to 96% negative ... chemistry acts by exclusion".
- The reanalysis climate product and years are unnamed (\todo), and XRF units and instrument are unconfirmed.

Fix:
- Treat non-detects as censored (indicator plus value, or Tobit/rank with ties), or drop axes with >50% non-detects.
- Use a permuted-axis negative control with matched spatial structure.
- Aggregate to site-visit or use mixed models with a site random effect.
- Recheck the exclusion asymmetry under CLR, or under normalisation to total mapped or total genome-resolved reads.
- Name the climate source.

LITERATURE AND STRONGEST SUPPORTED CONTRIBUTION

Against the downloaded literature, the generic dark-proteome conclusions are not new:
- FESNov (Rodríguez del Río 2024, full text verified): purifying selection, lineage restriction.
- Pavlopoulos 2023 and Aplakidou 2026: ecosystem restriction.
- TED (Lau 2024) and AFESM (Yeo 2025): fold space near saturation.

What is distinctive and currently well supported:
(1) The first genome-resolved, repeatedly sampled catalogue of a hyperarid sand sea: 5,438 species, all placed in GTDB phyla; a 237-species Thermoplasmatota radiation exceeding GTDB's representation of those classes; and a subsurface association of that radiation (40/45 sites).
(2) An explicitly controlled census, with matched annotated families, a known-fold floor, and a size-corrected reference-absence analysis. These controls are rare in this literature even with the fixes above.

The "dark proteins are the accessory genome of lineages without reference strains" thesis is the potentially high-impact contribution. It is not yet supported (R1-06, R1-08).
