## Neutral summary

The revision answers most of R2 with real analyses rather than rewording:
- **Raw-hit structural decomposition:** `structural_raw_audit/summary.json` reproduces 22/66 no-row models, 1,009/2,816 vs 35/2,947, 73/2,816 vs 2/2,947, and 797 gated local matches.
- **Stratum-level dN/dS:** the stratum bootstrap intervals and dark/control/overlap-weighted means (+0.012/−0.022/−0.018) match `selection_estimands/summary.json`, which root reproduced locally.
- **Full MIMAG reporting:** 126 high-quality, 157 with all three rRNAs, 3,629 with at least 18 tRNA types.
- **Two-rule taxonomy table:** both rows sum to 5,438.
- **Culture nucleotide test redone with base-level alignment:** the `-c` rerun gives the four cells 516/1,105, 4,877/16,056, 219/236 and 62,925/65,477. These match `summary.json` and supersede my R2 numbers, which rested on approximate PAF identities. Root's discovery of the missing `-c` was correct.
- **Gene-call screens:** AntiFam, an opposite-strand overlap screen (coordinates verified for 536,437/536,437 members across 550 samples, no read errors) and a Bakta locus diagnostic.
- **Other repairs:**
  - The environment table rebuilds byte-identically (`comparison.json`: SHA-256 equal).
  - Ecology has moved to a qualified Supplementary Note.
  - All 71 citation keys resolve.
  - The abstract is 142 words.

Three of the new analyses contain results that **change interpretation but are not yet drawn out in the text**:
1. **Culture comparison.** Among dark calls whose DNA is present in the cultured genome, 45.6% have no culture CDS at that locus. The figure for annotated calls is 0.9%. The dark-vs-annotated protein-sharing gap is therefore mostly a gene-calling difference at shared DNA, not strain variation.
2. **dN/dS below one does not exclude known spurious families.** Families flagged by AntiFam or the overlap screen have median dN/dS 0.76, and 77% are below one.
3. **The opposite-strand overlap screen has low power by construction.** Prodigal limits opposite-strand overlaps to 200 bp and forces a choice between heavily overlapping ORFs.

All three can be fixed with text and existing tables; no new computation is required. Remaining blockers are external: public data release, genome deposition and archive DOI, and three `\todo` markers.

## Findings

### R3-01. Major. [RECOMPUTED] The locus audit shows dark protein-sharing loss is mostly gene-calling disagreement; the text does not say so

**Location:**
- Abstract: "Culture-derived assemblies revealed DNA support for many dark calls lacking a reciprocal protein match."
- Results, l.213–218.
- Discussion, l.396–399: "Its interpretation depends on gene prediction ... as well as strain variation."

**Evidence** (`culture_nucleotide_cigar/per_gene.tsv` joined to `culture_locus/per_gene.tsv`, eligible calls ≥90 nt):

| | Dark | Annotated |
|---|---|---|
| Protein-shared | 236/1,341 (17.6%) | 65,477/81,533 (80.3%) |
| Protein-shared or DNA-supported | 752/1,341 (56.1%) | 70,354/81,533 (86.3%) |
| DNA present but no culture Bakta CDS over ≥50% of the aligned span | 343/752 (45.6%) | 606/70,354 (0.86%) |

- The 343 dark no-CDS calls are short: median 150 nt, 85.7% complete (partial code 00).
- The 92 "same-orientation" dark calls are mostly partial (10.9% complete; median 126 nt). That points to contig-edge fragments failing the reciprocal-coverage rule.
- Bakta predicts CDSs with Prodigal, then filters spurious CDSs and sORFs using AntiFam and overlap filters (Schwengers 2021, retained full text, verified passage). Identical DNA where Pyrodigal-meta calls a gene in the MAG but the culture annotation calls none therefore indicates caller- or filter-dependent ORFs. That is directly relevant to coding status.
- **Scope caveat:** these are all dark calls in 22 well-referenced bacterial MAGs, not the 106,042 admitted families. I could not check membership locally.

**Smallest remedy (existing artifacts):**
- Report the DNA-level sharing rates (56.1% vs 86.3%) and the no-CDS contrast (45.6% vs 0.86%) in Results.
- Replace the abstract sentence. Suggested wording: "In culture-derived genomes, many dark calls had identical DNA but no corresponding annotated gene."
- Rewrite Discussion l.396–399 so that gene-call disagreement is the leading explanation and strain variation is secondary.
- State how many of the 1,341 calls are members of admitted families (a coordinate join to `fam_cluster.tsv`, minutes of CPU).
- Optional: list any non-CDS Bakta features (tRNA, ncRNA, CRISPR) at the 343 loci from the same GFF3 files.

### R3-02. Major. [RECOMPUTED] Known-spurious families mostly pass the dN/dS < 1 criterion; this calibration is missing

**Location:**
- Results, l.183–188: removing 102 flagged families leaves median 0.4251.
- Abstract: "frame-dependent constraint".
- Discussion, l.384–386.

**Evidence** (`dnds.tsv` × `antifam/flagged_families.txt` × `shadow_overlap/family_flags.tsv`):

| Flag set | Evaluable families | Median dN/dS | IQR | Share below 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AntiFam | 47 | 0.831 | — | 76.6% |
| Opposite-strand overlap | 44 | 0.675 | — | 77.3% |
| Union | 91 | 0.762 | 0.53–0.97 | 76.9% |
| All dark families | 97,200 | 0.425 | — | — |

- The criterion separates flagged from unflagged families in part (median 0.76 vs 0.43), but most known-spurious families still fall below one.
- This fits theory: changes that are synonymous in one frame of an overlapping pair can be nonsynonymous in the other, which distorts dN/dS in overlapping frames (Nelson 2020 OLGenie, retained, verified passage).
- Removing the flagged families cannot change the overall median, so the reported sensitivity analysis is uninformative about whether the test discriminates.
- **Caveats:** a family is flagged if any one member matches, and n is small.

**Smallest remedy:**
- Report the flagged-family distribution beside the exclusion result.
- State that dN/dS < 1 is necessary but not sufficient for coding status.
- Phrase the abstract and Discussion as "lower dN/dS in the annotated than in a +1-shifted frame", not as constraint establishing coding status.
- Cite Nelson 2020 and Yooseph 2007; both are already retained.

### R3-03. Moderate. [SOURCE, RECOMPUTED] The overlap screen's null result is largely structural

**Location:** l.184–188 and Methods l.612–617.

**Evidence:**
- Hyatt 2010 (retained): Prodigal allows "200 bp overlap between 3' ends" on opposite strands and no 5'-end overlap. Its dynamic programming exists "to force the program to choose between two heavily overlapping ORFs".
- In `overlaps.tsv`, the maximum observed overlap is 195 nt and the longest flagged dark member is 381 nt.
- So the screen can flag only short calls in convergent arrangements. It cannot detect the main shadow-ORF case: the caller kept the dark ORF instead of the true gene on the other strand or frame.
- The current caveat ("do not detect every uncalled alternative ORF") understates this.

**Remedy:**
- State the Prodigal overlap limits.
- Describe the 70/46 counts as conditional on co-retention under those limits.
- Optional extension (CPU, IBEX): translate the opposite-strand and other frames of the member loci and search them against UniRef50, as in the Yooseph 2007 shadow filter.

### R3-04. Moderate. [CONFIRMED] Persistence is still used as evidence outside the abstract, and the Introduction's control description is inaccurate

- **Discussion, l.382–383:** "Evidence is strongest for the recurrent subset ... 79.4% recruit reads in all five expeditions." Results (l.148) correctly says a prevalence-conditioned null is missing.
- **Supplementary Note, l.919–922:** "persist more than the average dark family (86.8%, 89.8% vs 79.4%)", with no prevalence adjustment.
- **Introduction, l.80:** "matched annotated families for recurrence". Control families were admitted without the sample/site gate (l.572), and no control persistence is reported.
- **Remedy:** drop 79.4% from the Discussion lead or label it as prevalence-dependent detection; qualify the S1 comparison the same way; fix l.80.

### R3-05. Moderate. [RECOMPUTED] The rarefaction claim is generalised beyond the per-catalogue data, and the control arm reveals calibration error

**Location:**
- Abstract: "Soil- and genome-derived catalogues recovered more dark families than ... expectation."
- Discussion, l.413–416: "random MGnify90 subset"; "Comparable gains in other genome-derived catalogues".
- Fig. 5b.

**Evidence** (`content_vs_depth.tsv`; gap is expected minus observed absence, in points):

| Catalogue | Dark gap | Control gap |
|---|---|---|
| Atacama | −4.67 | −29.97 |
| Desert autotroph | +17.42 | — |
| Arid | +28.38 | — |
| Antarctic | +9.47 | −3.60 |
| Mojave | +13.60 | — |
| Five deserts combined | +24.33 | +0.93 |
| GEM (genome-derived) | +4.27 | — |
| SPIRE | +21.30 | +1.23 |

- The Atacama control point sits 30 points off the diagonal, which shows the size-only model is poorly calibrated for small catalogues.
- GEM's gain is small, which contradicts "comparable gains in other genome-derived catalogues".

**Remedy:**
- Name the catalogues in the abstract: the combined desert set and SPIRE.
- Report control-arm residuals as the model's calibration and comment on the Atacama outlier in the Fig. 5b caption.
- Replace "random MGnify90 subset" in l.413 with the analytic-expectation wording already used in Results.

## Strongest defensible contribution

1. **A resource:** the first genome-resolved, repeatedly sampled catalogue of a hyperarid sand sea.
2. **A descriptive lineage result:** a robust, quality- and abundance-matched Thermoplasmatota darkness contrast, 236 of 237 above the bacterial 95th percentile.
3. **A controlled demonstration** that dark-proteome inferences depend on gene calling, controls and estimands:
   - DNA-present dark calls lack a culture gene about 50 times more often than annotated calls;
   - known-spurious families still mostly show dN/dS < 1;
   - structural "novelty" depends on normalisation and retrieval;
   - constraint contrasts depend on divergence weighting.

Point 3 is now a concrete, quantitative methodological message and is arguably the paper's most transferable result.
