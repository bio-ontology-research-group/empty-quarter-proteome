# Trace-gas scavenging in hyper-arid deserts — prior literature

Curated 2026-06-16 for the Empty Quarter MAGs paper, to position our finding (3,386 MAGs / 22% high-affinity
atmospheric H₂; 3,828 / 25% form-I CO; Group-1l dominant) against what is already known. PDFs (open-access
only) are in this folder; full bibliographic detail in `downloads_manifest.tsv`. **23 OA PDFs downloaded,
5 paywalled (recorded, not redistributed).**

> PDFs are git-ignored (copyright + repo size); the manifest + this README are versioned. To version the
> PDFs too, remove `*.pdf` from `.gitignore` here.

## The headline gap
**No trace-gas / atmospheric-chemosynthesis study exists for the Rub' al-Khali or any Arabian desert**, and
none for a *hot sand desert* at this scale. The field is built on **cold deserts** (Antarctic Dry Valleys)
and **high-altitude / hyper-arid** systems (Atacama). The Empty Quarter is genuine whitespace — our census is
the first genome-resolved atmospheric-trace-gas guild for the world's largest sand desert.

## 1. Desert atmospheric chemosynthesis — the core precedent
- **Ji 2017, *Nature*** (paywalled) — the founding paper: Antarctic Dry Valley soils support **primary production
  from atmospheric H₂ + CO** ("atmospheric chemosynthesis"). Our direct conceptual antecedent.
- **Ray 2022, *ISME J*** — atmospheric chemosynthesis generalised across cold deserts.
- **Ray 2023, *MMBR*** — review of atmospheric chemosynthesis (best OA review; substitutes Greening & Grinter 2022 NRM).
- **Bay 2021, *Nat Microbiol*** (paywalled) — trace-gas oxidisers are widespread across soils globally.
- **Bay 2020, *Front Microbiol*** — chemosynthesis capacity across polar deserts.

## 2. Hot / hyper-arid desert energetics (Atacama, high-altitude)
- **Ortiz 2021, *PNAS*** — multiple energy sources (incl. trace gases) sustain Atacama/Antarctic desert microbes.
- **Leung 2020, *mSystems*** — energetic basis of microbial persistence in desert soil (Namib/global; trace-gas central).
- **Jordaan 2020, *mSystems*** — H₂-oxidisers abundant in desert soils, **activity stimulated by hydration** (the moisture-dependence directly relevant to our climate-feedback discussion).
- **Lynch 2014, *Front Microbiol*** — high-elevation Atacama/Andes Actinobacteria with trace-gas metabolism (our dominant phylum).
- **Schulze-Makuch 2018, *PNAS*** — hyper-arid Atacama as a transitory (rain-pulsed) microbial habitat.
- **Atacama 2024 review** — current synthesis of the Atacama living microbiome.

## 3. Enzymology that defines "high-affinity / atmospheric"
- **Greening 2016, *ISME J*** — hydrogenase distribution survey (the group framework we classify into).
- **Søndergaard 2016, *Sci Rep*** — **HydDB**, the classifier we used for the EQ hydB set.
- **Greening 2015, *PNAS*** — Acidobacteria persist by trace-gas scavenging (a guild phylum we recover).
- **Grinter 2023, *Nature*** — cryo-EM structure of **Huc**, the group-1h atmospheric-H₂ hydrogenase.
- **Constant 2016, *MicrobiologyOpen*** — *Streptomyces* high-affinity [NiFe]-hydrogenase (OA stand-in for Constant 2008/2010).
- **Cordero 2019, *ISME J*** — atmospheric **CO** oxidation supports survival; source of our form-I CoxL references + threshold.
- **King & Weber 2007, *Nat Rev Microbiol*** (paywalled) — aerobic CO-oxidiser review.

## 4. Global biogeochemistry + climate relevance
- **Conrad 1996, *Microbiol Rev*** — soil microbes as the controllers of atmospheric trace-gas budgets (the classic).
- **Tveit 2019, *PNAS*** — USCα bacterium oxidising **atmospheric methane** (high-affinity CH₄, a third trace gas).
- **Ehhalt & Rohrer 2009, *Tellus B*** — tropospheric H₂ cycle; soil uptake as dominant sink.
- **Buzzard 2022, *PeerJ*** — semiarid soil H₂ uptake vs moisture (mechanism for the dryland sink).
- **Ocko & Hamburg 2022, *ACP*** — climate consequences of H₂ emissions (indirect GWP).
- **Bertagni 2022, *Nat Commun*** — hydrogen-economy leakage raises atmospheric methane.
- **Paulot 2021, *Int J Hydrogen Energy*** (paywalled) — global H₂ model incl. soil removal + radiative forcing.

## How we use this
1. **Novelty framing**: every precedent is a cold or high-altitude desert — none is a hot sand desert, none is Arabian.
2. **Methods provenance**: HydDB (Søndergaard 2016) + Cordero 2019 are the citable bases of our classification.
3. **Climate discussion**: Conrad 1996, Ehhalt 2009, Ocko 2022, Bertagni 2022, Paulot 2021 anchor the global-sink / indirect-GWP / hydrogen-economy argument; Jordaan 2020 + Buzzard 2022 anchor the moisture-feedback caveat.
4. **The Group-1l angle**: desert dominance of 1l (vs the 1h "Huc" of Grinter 2023 / mesic soils) is under-described in this literature — a contribution, not just confirmation.
