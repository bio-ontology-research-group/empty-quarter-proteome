\section*{Abstract}
The physiological capacities of archaea in hyperarid soils remain poorly resolved because many of their genes cannot be connected to known functions. We used repeated metagenomic sampling across the Rub' al-Khali to ask how this unknown repertoire is distributed among organisms and cellular roles. Thermoplasmatota were widespread across the transect and enriched in shallow subsurface soils in paired comparisons. Although they comprised 4.4\% of species representatives, they accounted for 19.8\% of recurrent sequence-dark families assigned to a single phylum. Coding-potential and evolutionary analyses supported the biological credibility of many of these families. Within Thermoplasmatota, sequence-dark genes were predominantly assigned to accessory orthogroups and more frequently carried predicted export signals than non-dark genes. The export association persisted after adjustment for protein length and lineage composition, whereas comparisons with related external genomes did not establish excess sequence darkness specific to this desert. Most confidently modelled dark proteins matched known structural folds. A screen of 1,460 families identified two conserved local gene modules consistent with cell-surface roles, although their context associations weakened under genus-restricted testing and their biochemical functions remain unresolved. Metabolic-model and viral analyses constrained alternative hypotheses. These observations identify predicted export as a substantial unresolved component of widespread desert archaea and prioritise specific modules for testing substrate acquisition and surface organisation. \texttt{[PENDING:\allowbreak EXT-COMP-HARMON; harmonised external export comparison.]}

\section*{Introduction}

Microbial life in the Rub' al-Khali remains poorly characterised despite the desert's extent and severe water limitation. Repeated sampling across its landscape can establish which organisms recur across sites and soil compartments, and genome reconstruction can connect their occurrence to encoded capacities. Our companion ecological study examines community replacement and functional differences across these habitats \citep{rubalkhali_datapaper,tawfiq2026companion}. Here we focus on the unresolved protein repertoire of the archaea recovered from the same survey.

Unknown genes are not unique to desert archaea. Maeke and colleagues recovered a \emph{Ca.} Penumbrarchaeia genome from a protein-amended anoxic marine enrichment and assembled a 35-genome comparative dataset through extensive data mining and additional sampling, identifying a substantial uncharacterised repertoire \citep{maeke2025thermoplasmatota}. Their results provide evidence relevant to protein use in those marine lineages; they do not establish the physiology of the Thermoplasmatota found in the Empty Quarter. Moreover, annotation-defined unknown proteins and proteins lacking a UniRef50 sequence match are different categories. The scientific question is therefore how the unresolved genes are organised in our organisms and which functions can be supported for particular families.

Several explanations must be distinguished. Unfamiliar sequences may reflect limited reference sampling, divergent versions of established functions, or incorrect and incomplete gene predictions. Structural comparisons often recover relationships that sequence searches miss, including across archaeal lineages \citep{sussfeld2025divergent,schaeffer2026archaeal}. A recognised fold can narrow the possibilities without establishing substrate specificity or cellular role. Conversely, the absence of a structural match does not by itself identify a novel fold. Conserved architecture, functional residues, evolutionary distribution and genomic context provide complementary evidence for evaluating individual families.

We first establish the occurrence and encoded metabolic context of Thermoplasmatota across the Rub' al-Khali. We then test whether their elevated sequence-dark fraction is supported by coding and evolutionary evidence and whether it is associated with gene-family prevalence and predicted export. Finally, we use existing structural and neighbourhood evidence to identify specific functional hypotheses.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Widespread Thermoplasmatota are enriched below the soil surface}

The study comprises 551 shotgun metagenomes collected during five campaigns along a 1,043~km transect. The shared resource describes the specimens, sequencing records and 5,438 species-representative genomes \citep{rubalkhali_datapaper} (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). Thermoplasmatota accounted for 237 representatives. In the original catalogue mapping they were detected in 482 of 550 non-zero profiles, despite a low median relative abundance. This combination establishes broad occurrence across the sampled landscape, rather than dominance of individual communities.

Competitive mapping against the desert catalogue and external Thermoplasmatota references supported the depth association (Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}a). At 20\% genome breadth, relative abundance was higher in the shallow subsurface at 35 of 36 paired sites, with an average increase of 0.53 percentage points of total reads (95\% spatial-block bootstrap CI 0.26--0.90). The direction was retained under a 50\% breadth requirement. The original mapping covered 37 paired sites and is shown separately as a sensitivity analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}b). The MAPQ~20 sensitivity was less decisive: its raw-read interval included zero. We therefore describe a depth association supported by the primary and breadth-filtered mappings, with its dependence on read-assignment criteria retained in the Supplement (Supplementary Notes S3 and S6).

Annotated genes provide a metabolic context for this occurrence pattern. The recovered genomes contained marker sets for central carbon metabolism, including glycolysis and the glyoxylate cycle, and for amino-acid and peptide transport (Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}c). The two canonical archaeal carbon-fixation cycles shown in the figure were not recovered under the specified module-coverage rule. These observations are compatible with heterotrophic capacities, but incomplete MAGs and incomplete annotation limit interpretation of missing modules.

The complete microTrait analysis of the shared genome resource \citep{rubalkhali_datapaper,karaoz2022microtrait} refined this metabolic picture. All 237 Thermoplasmatota satisfied at least one free-amino-acid transport rule, whereas only ten satisfied a peptide-transport rule (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:microtrait_thermo}). Protein-degradation categories were also widespread, but include intracellular peptidases and protein-processing systems. These annotations support amino-acid acquisition and protein processing as components of the encoded repertoire, while extracellular protein use remains a family-specific hypothesis for the exported unknown proteins. Sparse carbon-fixation marker calls depended on the annotation rule and are reported separately from complete pathway reconstruction.

\subsection*{The elevated unknown fraction is supported by coding and evolutionary evidence}

We define sequence-dark proteins operationally as catalogue representatives without a UniRef50 match under the stated search criterion. This describes reference coverage, not an experimental determination that their function is unknown. Representative-genome genes inherit this classification through the documented catalogue reconciliation; genes without a reconciled label are excluded from dark-versus-non-dark denominators.

The median dark fraction among labelled genes was 30.4\% in Thermoplasmatota, compared with 4.0\% in other archaea and 5.4\% in bacteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:credibility}a). The contrast persisted when restricted to complete calls of at least 100 amino acids. Thermoplasmatota also contributed 19.8\% of recurrent dark families assigned to one phylum, while comprising 4.4\% of species representatives.

The 19.8\% single-phylum family attribution uses the frozen analytical taxonomy snapshot (1,898 of 5,438 whole taxonomy strings, including 247 phylum labels, differ from the canonical foundational GTDB release 232 table, while all 5,438 domain labels and all 237 Thermoplasmatota representative IDs agree); insert canonical-taxonomy family-attribution sensitivity.]\par}

Several independent analyses supported coding potential and sequence constraint among recurrent families. RNAcode detected reading-frame coding signatures across protein-length strata, whereas column-permuted alignments rarely passed the same criterion (Fig.~\ref{fig:credibility}b). Family-level between-assembly dN/dS and within-population pN/pS were predominantly below one in the evaluable subsets (Fig.~\ref{fig:credibility}c,d). Applying the recurrence criteria to dinucleotide-shuffled assemblies recovered only one family, which matched none of the observed dark families. These results support the biological credibility of much of the recurrent repertoire. Full cohort definitions and complementary validation analyses are reported in Supplementary Note S1.

\subsection*{Unknown genes are associated with accessory prevalence and predicted export}

Sequence constraint within a family does not imply that the family is part of a species-wide core. Across the 237 Thermoplasmatota representatives, most labelled dark genes belonged to shell or cloud orthogroups; only 0.8\% belonged to orthogroups present in at least 90\% of the genomes (Fig.~\ref{fig:export}a). This prevalence classification depends on the sampled lineages and orthogroup definition.

Predicted export was also associated with sequence darkness. SignalP identified signal peptides in 32.5\% of labelled dark Thermoplasmatota genes, compared with 11.6\% of non-dark genes (Fig.~\ref{fig:export}b). This is a gene-level comparison within the same genome cohort.

The export association persisted after adjustment for protein length, genome quality, contig-edge status and taxonomic order. Using the foundational taxonomy, the adjusted odds ratio was 9.12 (95\% genus-clustered CI 8.54--9.73); restricting the analysis to complete interior coding sequences gave a similar association (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:export_adj_completed}). These estimates used 19 named genera and 135 unclassified-genome singleton groups for uncertainty estimation. The latter are resampling groups, not established independent evolutionary lineages. Within-genome comparisons and the older taxonomy snapshot gave concordant results.

Direct searches against the same UniRef50 release separated the desert cohort from the full external reference set, but this difference was sensitive to lineage composition. Restricting the comparison to shared named orders reduced the difference in mean per-genome darkness to 1.35 percentage points (95\% genus-or-singleton block interval $-0.32$ to 3.17). Comparisons restricted to closer relatives were similarly uncertain (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:ext_uniref50_completed}). These searches harmonised the reference database and search rule; the existing proteomes had different gene-calling histories. They therefore do not establish an excess of sequence darkness specific to the Rub' al-Khali.

\subsection*{Conserved neighbourhoods identify candidate roles at the cell surface}

Remote-profile and structural searches recovered relationships for many sequence-dark families (Fig.~\ref{fig:structural_main}; Supplementary Note S2). Most dark models meeting the specified confidence and length criteria matched known PDB folds. The all-family ESMFold screen and Thermoplasmatota-focused AlphaFold2 rescue represent separate selected cohorts (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:structall}). These relationships allowed us to ask whether genomic context and protein architecture converge on particular cellular roles.

We tested six context-defined roles across 1,460 candidate and comparison families, then examined 47 families in detail (Supplementary Note S8). Two exported dark families occurred in conserved, immediately adjacent gene pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:surface_modules}). One was adjacent to a second dark family carrying a PilJ-family profile in 13 loci; the other was adjacent to a partner with cell-envelope-related profiles in 11 loci. All 24 pairs were co-oriented, with short intergenic gaps or overlaps, supporting conserved local modules. The associated neighbourhoods span six and five genus-or-singleton groups, respectively. Their context associations passed the genome-level screen, but genus-restricted tests across the complete family-by-role universe remained uncertain. The partner annotations and local organisation therefore prioritise cell-surface hypotheses for these families; their precise roles remain unresolved.

The detailed audit also separated plausible alternatives from apparent functional matches. A sterol-carrier-family profile and structural match favoured a lipid-transfer fold over a proposed tRNA-charging role for one gap-filling candidate (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:cand_audit_completed}). For the two surface-associated leads, available seed models matched their source sequences, but the independently verified interior representatives lacked structural models. Neither seed yielded a qualifying match to the tested pilin or surface-layer reference panel. Across the 47-family follow-up, 326 of 428 examined loci passed complete-interior coding-sequence checks, and 27 of those loci had an exact-sequence structural model. The combined evidence narrows candidates to conserved modules and fold-level alternatives, while leaving biochemical assignment open.

\subsection*{Metabolic and viral tests constrain alternative explanations}

We next asked whether metabolic deficits or viral interactions could connect unknown proteins to specific functions. Across 484 desert and reference Thermoplasmatota genomes, predicted reaction requirements depended strongly on the biomass composition assumed by the model (Supplementary Note S8). Removing seven non-$F_{420}$ cofactors from the default methanogen-derived biomass template reduced the median number of added reactions by 73 in the evaluable desert models under the anoxic organic-acid and amino-acid medium. In seven high-completeness reference genomes, masking known genes and attempting to recover their reactions gave a mean recovery of only 16.8\%; alternative reactions comprised 76.7\% of newly induced additions. This benchmark supports using gap-filling to formulate nutrient and pathway hypotheses, with family-specific assignments requiring additional sequence and contextual evidence.

A CRISPR-spacer screen of all 591 archaeal representatives recovered five sequence-qualified host--virus candidate pairs involving four hosts, including one Thermoplasmatota host (Supplementary Note S8). These links support candidate historical exposure to viral sequences. The sparse links and incomplete spacer recovery limited tests of association with the unknown protein repertoire. Together, the metabolic and viral analyses leave the conserved surface-associated modules as concrete candidates for further functional work, with substrate acquisition, envelope organisation and other cellular roles still distinguishable hypotheses.

\section*{Discussion}

Repeated sampling places an unusually large sequence-dark repertoire within archaea that recur across the Rub' al-Khali and are associated with its shallow subsurface soils. Coding and evolutionary evidence supports the credibility of many unknown families. Their association with accessory gene prevalence and predicted export identifies a biologically organised part of the encoded repertoire that remains poorly connected to function.

Predicted export organises the unresolved repertoire around several possible cellular roles. Maeke and colleagues linked marine \emph{Ca.} Penumbrarchaeia to protein-amended enrichments \citep{maeke2025thermoplasmatota}; our genome content and occurrence data provide a different kind of evidence. Peptide transporters and predicted export are compatible with substrate acquisition, but surface organisation and cellular maintenance remain alternatives for individual dark families. Because the export enrichment persists within genomes and across taxonomic orders while comparisons with related external genomes did not establish an excess of sequence darkness in the desert cohort, the central biological question is the cellular role of the exported repertoire rather than desert-specific sequence novelty; external SignalP comparisons remain pending.

The structural results also argue for family-specific interpretation. Work on divergent archaeal proteins shows how known folds can support informative functional hypotheses when architecture, residues and evolutionary relationships are examined together \citep{sussfeld2025divergent,schaeffer2026archaeal}. A large number of recovered structural matches is therefore a means of narrowing candidate functions. It is not a substitute for evaluating the proposed role of each family.

The conserved gene pairs give the export association a more specific biological context. Their organisation supports coordinated cellular roles, while the differences between partner annotations leave substrate acquisition and envelope organisation as distinct hypotheses. The low masked-reaction recovery and strong dependence on biomass composition explain why metabolic gap-filling alone offered little resolution between these possibilities. The independent soil proteogenomic dataset also lacked representatives of the focal archaeal class and the complete protein-search background needed to evaluate the two leads (Supplementary Note S8). Functional interpretation therefore rests on the convergence of localisation, sequence, structure and genomic context for each candidate module.

The conclusions remain limited by incomplete genomes, reference-dependent classification and computational localisation and function predictions. We cannot infer translation, secretion or substrate consumption from these data. Within those limits, the survey identifies predicted exported capacity as a substantial unresolved component of widespread desert archaea and provides the sequence, evolutionary and ecological context needed to investigate it.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Sampling and genome reconstruction}

We analysed 551 soil samples from 59 transect sites and two auxiliary locations in the Saudi Rub' al-Khali. Five expeditions between March 2023 and October 2025 sampled surface soil (0~cm), subsurface soil (5--10~cm) and root-adjacent soil (5--10~cm) \citep{rubalkhali_datapaper,tawfiq2026companion}. Site, visit and soil-position coverage was unbalanced. The sample manifest identifies 117 surface, 166 subsurface and 268 root-adjacent samples (Suppl. Fig.~\ref{fig:design}). DNA extraction used PowerLyzer PowerSoil or DNeasy PowerSoil Pro kits. Illumina NovaSeq 6000 and NovaSeq X sequencing produced 554 paired-end runs, including two runs for each of three samples. Field handling, library preparation, read filtering and provenance limitations are detailed in Supplementary Methods.

Reads were filtered with fastp v1.0.1 \citep{chen2018fastp} and assembled per sample with MEGAHIT v1.2.9 \citep{li2015megahit}; 549 samples yielded assemblies. Two SemiBin2 v2.2.1 binning runs \citep{pan2023semibin2} used pretrained and self-supervised models, and DAS\_Tool v1.1.7 \citep{sieber2018dastool} reconciled their outputs into 14,426 bins. CheckM2 v1.1.0 \citep{chklovski2023checkm2} identified 12,434 bins with at least 50\% completeness and at most 10\% contamination. dRep v3.6.2 \citep{olm2017drep} dereplicated these at primary and secondary ANI thresholds of 90\% and 95\%; one bin was dropped internally, leaving 5,438 species representatives (4,847 bacterial and 591 archaeal, including 237 Thermoplasmatota and 354 other archaea; Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}).

GTDB-Tk v2.7.2 \citep{chaumeil2022gtdbtk} classified representatives against GTDB release 232 \citep{parks2022gtdb}. Taxonomic resolution was summarised by the first unassigned rank, separately from the presence of placeholder names (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:naming}). Across all 5,438 species representatives, the frozen analytical taxonomy snapshot (\texttt{v2\_reps.tsv.gz} and \texttt{genome\_quality.tsv}) and the canonical foundational table (\texttt{gtdb\_r232\_classification} in \texttt{refined\_representatives.tsv}) share identical genome identifiers, quality metrics, domain assignments (4,847 bacterial and 591 archaeal) and the same 237 Thermoplasmatota representatives (185 \texttt{c\_\_SW-10-69-26}, 51 \texttt{c\_\_E2} and 1 \texttt{c\_\_Thermoplasmata}), whereas 1,898 whole taxonomy strings differ across the catalogue (247 phylum, 34 class, 220 order and 1,124 genus labels), including 42 of the 237 Thermoplasmatota strings (canonical foundational metadata: 4 named orders plus 2 genomes unassigned at order rank, and 19 named genera plus 135 genomes unassigned at genus rank; legacy analytical snapshot: 4 order labels, and 14 named genera plus 139 genomes unassigned at genus rank). barrnap v0.9 and tRNAscan-SE v2.0.12 \citep{chan2021trnascan} supplied rRNA and tRNA assessments for full MIMAG classification \citep{bowers2017mimag}. Under the strict MIMAG medium-quality boundary ($<10\%$ contamination), 126 representatives are high quality, 5,311 are strict medium quality and one representative (\texttt{48Dr2\_\_self\_SemiBin\_25}, 64.61\% completeness and 10.0\% contamination) sits on the upper contamination boundary, yielding 126 high-quality and 5,312 non-high-quality representatives under the inclusive dRep threshold ($\leq 10\%$ contamination; distinguished from the 2,259 genomes meeting completeness $\geq 90\%$ and contamination $\leq 5\%$). GUNC v1.1.1 \citep{orakov2021gunc} against ProGenomes 3 screened all 12,434 deposited bins; among the 5,438 representatives, 4,319 passed, 837 were flagged and 282 could not be scored because of low reference representation. The frozen dark-fraction sensitivity analysis in Supplementary Note S2 used a separate ProGenomes 2.1 screen of the representatives, in which 462 failed the default criterion.

\subsection*{Protein clusters and recurrent unknown families}

Pyrodigal v3.7.1 \citep{larralde2022pyrodigal,hyatt2010prodigal} predicted genes in metagenomic mode, retaining partial calls. The protein catalogue used 552 assembly files, comprising the 549 canonical assemblies and three additional library assemblies. MMseqs2 v18.8cc5c \citep{steinegger2017mmseqs2,steinegger2018linclust} clustered 753,305,533 protein predictions at 90\% identity with 80\% bidirectional coverage into 449,265,306 representatives. Searches against UniRef50 2026\_01 \citep{suzek2015uniref} used sensitivity 7.5, $E\leq 10^{-3}$ and at most ten returned sequences, with no additional identity or coverage threshold. A representative lacking a hit defined a sequence-dark cluster.

A cluster was genome-linked when any member occurred on a contig belonging to one of the 37,949 quality-filtered bins from the two unrefined binnings. This defined 43,423,322 genome-linked representatives, including 4,292,446 dark representatives (9.89\%; Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). MMseqs2 clustering at 50\% identity and 80\% bidirectional coverage grouped the dark representatives into families. Admission required at least three distinct 90\%-identity representatives assembled across three samples and two sites. This yielded 106,042 recurrent families containing 536,437 member representatives.

The UniRef50-matched control pool came from the same genome-linked universe. Controls were sampled by protein length, clustered identically and selected to match family-size bins. Control admission required member count; the sample and site recurrence gates applied to dark families. After admission, median query lengths were 105 residues for dark families and 75 for controls. Length-stratified comparisons and matched reference panels address specified aspects of these differences. Detailed selection rules, dN/dS estimation, shifted-frame controls, AntiFam searches, overlap screening and culture comparisons are given in Supplementary Note S1 and Supplementary Methods.

Four further tests addressed coding status and gene prediction. RNAcode v0.3.1 \citep{washietl2011rnacode} scored codon alignments of dark and control families whose representative was shorter than 100 residues, and of all longer families. Each alignment contained the representative and up to 29 further distinct member sequences, aligned as proteins with MAFFT v7.525 \citep{katoh2013mafft} and back-translated. A family was coding when the best high-scoring segment in the annotated frame had $p<0.05$; a column-permuted copy of each alignment supplied the null. For the gene-call reconciliation (\texttt{per\_genome\_reconciliation.tsv}), 20,451,986 Pyrodigal genes predicted in the 5,438 species representatives (\texttt{EQ-PROT-PYRODIGAL-REP-v1.0.0}; distinct from the 19,186,792 Bakta release proteins in \texttt{EQ-PROT-BAKTA-v1.0.0}) were matched to catalogue calls by contig, coordinates, strand and sequence, and 20,216,302 inherited the dark or non-dark label of the matched catalogue cluster (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:genecall}). Unlabelled gene calls (235,684 across the catalogue; 10,521 in Thermoplasmatota) are excluded from dark-versus-non-dark denominators, and inherited catalogue labels are kept distinct from direct UniRef50 searches. For a null model of spurious calls, every contig of the 552 assemblies was shuffled with dinucleotide composition preserved (Easel \texttt{esl-shuffle -d} from HMMER v3.4 \citep{eddy2011hmmer}, seed 20261103) and gene calling repeated with the catalogue settings. Shuffled proteins on binned contigs then passed through the 90\%- and 50\%-identity clustering and the recurrence gates used for the dark families. For expression, 175 metatranscriptome runs from ten public desert and dryland studies (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:mt}) were obtained from the NCBI SRA open-data mirror; all reads were searched with DIAMOND v2.2.8 \texttt{blastx} against the translated members of all dark and control families (at least 80\% identity over at least 80\% of the read, best hit), and a family was transcribed when at least two reads of one run matched it. Read-level pN/pS used Bowtie2 v2.5.5 \citep{langmead2012bowtie2} alignments of each sample's reads to all dark and control family member genes and inStrain v1.9.1 \citep{olm2021instrain} gene profiles (Supplementary Methods).

\subsection*{Lineage assignment, abundance and ecological comparisons}

In the reconciled representative gene analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:credibility}a; \texttt{per\_genome\_reconciliation.tsv}), the per-genome dark fraction was the number of dark-labelled Pyrodigal calls divided by all labelled calls (\texttt{n\_dark / n\_labelled}, or \texttt{complete\_ge100\_n\_dark / complete\_ge100\_n\_labelled} for complete calls of at least 100 residues). An earlier assembly-cluster contig tally (\texttt{v2\_reps.tsv.gz}) is retained in Supplementary Note S2 and Suppl. Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage}. Family attribution traced original members through contigs and refined bins to species representatives using the frozen analytical taxonomy snapshot. Of 78,727 attributable recurrent families in that snapshot, 77,069 belonged to a single phylum; phylum enrichment was the share of these single-phylum families divided by the phylum's share of species representatives, with canonical-taxonomy attribution sensitivity tracked under \texttt{[PENDING:\allowbreak TAXONOMY-\allowbreak SNAPSHOT-\allowbreak SENSITIVITY]}. Genome-quality matching and phylogenetic analyses are specified in Supplementary Note S2 and Supplementary Methods.

CoverM v0.7.0 \citep{aroney2025coverm}, using minimap2 short-read mapping and default genome-mode filters, estimated relative abundance against the 5,438 representatives (\texttt{EQ-COVERM-REL-ABUND-v1.0.0}). Detection required non-zero abundance, including the default requirement that at least 10\% of a genome be covered. The analysis used 550 usable profiles from 551 samples after excluding the wholly unmapped \texttt{F5Dr1} library (in the shared cohort hierarchy \citep{rubalkhali_datapaper}, excluding the four non-core Trip~1 libraries \texttt{62PRr2}, \texttt{64Dr3}, \texttt{64PRr2} and \texttt{64Sr2} yields 546 core-transect profiles across 59 core sites, excluding \texttt{F45PRr3} with missing post-QC read-depth metadata yields 545, and matching to the 1,186 deduplicated 16S rRNA profiles yields 442 matched libraries across 58 core sites and 441 with complete read-depth metadata). Thermoplasmatota abundance was summed over its 237 representatives. We analysed both the percentage of all reads and the percentage of reads assigned to the representative catalogue.

For soil-position contrasts in the original mapping (Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}b), we averaged replicate profiles within each site, visit and compartment. We paired compartments within the same site and visit, then averaged the paired differences across visits within each site. Sites therefore contributed equally to inference. Surface--subsurface pairing yielded 69 site--visit pairs at 37 sites; root-adjacent--subsurface pairing yielded 107 pairs at 48 sites. Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests operated on site-level differences. Holm adjustment covered the four contrasts from two compartment pairs and two abundance denominators. Median-difference intervals used 10,000 site bootstrap resamples (seed 20261020); omission of each expedition in turn assessed visit sensitivity (Supplementary Note S3).

Competitive remapping of archaeal occurrence (\texttt{EQ-COVERM-ARCHAEA-COMP-v1.0.0}; Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}a) combined all 5,438 catalogue genomes with 301 external Thermoplasmatota genomes. Minimap2 short-read mapping of 193 paired surface and subsurface profiles from 68 visits at 36 core sites used paired FASTQs, at least 95\% read identity and 75\% aligned read length, with supplementary alignments excluded. CoverM provided original-read-denominator relative abundances, mean coverage and covered bases. Genome contributions were retained at breadth thresholds of 10\%, 20\% and 50\%. Replicates were averaged within visits and visit differences within sites. Mean-difference intervals resampled ten contiguous spatial blocks 2,000 times, with seed 20261108. A MAPQ~20 sensitivity used coverage from the filtered BAMs (Supplementary Note S6).

Separate gene-level recruitment mapped all 551 samples to the 536,437 dark-family member genes. Family detection required at least one member with covered fraction of 0.5; family abundance was summed member TPM. Occupancy comparisons retained protein-length strata. Independent assembly recurrence counted expeditions contributing an assembled family member. Visit-bundle permutations preserved sampling structure when assessing all-expedition detection (Supplementary Note S1).

\subsection*{Encoded metabolism, pangenome partitions and external reference comparisons}

Metabolic marker sets in Fig.~\ref{fig:ecology}c used the retained representative KEGG orthologue table (\texttt{04\_functional/repko\_clean/results/rep\_ko\_v2.tsv}), derived from Bakta v1.12.0 \citep{schwengers2021bakta} and eggNOG-mapper v2.1.13 \citep{cantalapiedra2021eggnog} annotations in which representatives emitted by DAS\_Tool refinement as strict contig subsets of a parent bin inherited annotations restricted to their retained contigs (Supplementary Methods). Although this table covers the same 5,438 representative genome identifiers as the foundational 19,186,792-protein Bakta annotation product (\texttt{EQ-PROT-BAKTA-v1.0.0}), byte- or sequence-level equivalence between the two products has not been established. KEGG module detection required at least two thirds of top-level steps, each satisfied by a listed alternative orthologue. Custom marker sets required explicitly defined subunit counts (Supplementary Methods). Peptidases and transporters were assigned by DIAMOND v2.2.8 searches against MEROPS \citep{rawlings2018merops} and TCDB \citep{saier2021tcdb} ($E\leq 10^{-10}$, at least 40\% identity and 70\% coverage), and carbohydrate-active enzymes with dbCAN v5.2.9 \citep{zheng2023dbcan}. gapseq v2.1.0 \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq} reconstructed metabolic models for the 237 desert and 301 external Thermoplasmatota genomes with the archaeal biomass definition and gap-filled each on its predicted medium. OrthoFinder v3.2 \citep{emms2019orthofinder} inferred orthogroups for the desert genomes alone and together with the external genomes; core, shell and cloud orthogroups occurred in at least 90\%, 15--90\% and fewer than 15\% of genomes.

External comparisons used frozen Thermoplasmatota proteomes from the desert representatives and public references. External proteins had been called with Prodigal in single-genome mode, with metagenomic-mode fallback, whereas the desert proteins came from metagenomic-mode calls. We removed MGnify entries representing the same assembly as a GTDB reference or sharing at least 99.9\% skani ANI with one, retaining 247 external genomes alongside 237 desert genomes. All proteomes were searched against UniRef50 release 2026\_01 with the same MMseqs2 parameters (sensitivity 7.5, $E$-value threshold $10^{-3}$ and at most ten accepted hits). We compared per-genome fractions without a qualifying match across the full set, a genome-quality subset, shared named orders and a closer-relative subset defined by AAI of at least 65\%. These are relative-matching criteria, not species assignments. Intervals used 1,000 genus-or-singleton block bootstrap draws (seed 20261108). Complete-ORF and legacy-taxonomy sensitivities are reported in Supplementary Note~S7 and Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:ext_uniref50_completed}. The searches harmonise reference and search criteria, but retain different gene-calling histories; external export predictions remain pending.

geNomad v1.13.0 \citep{camargo2024genomad} identified plasmid and virus sequences in all representatives. SignalP 6.0h \citep{teufel2022signalp6} (fast mode, organism group \texttt{other}) and TMbed \citep{bernhofer2022tmbed} predicted signal peptides and transmembrane segments for all Thermoplasmatota proteins and recurrent dark family members. Within the desert Thermoplasmatota, we modelled predicted export as a function of sequence darkness using grouped binomial models. Covariates were protein-length category, genome completeness and contamination, contig-edge status and taxonomic order. The seven length categories were $<100$, 100--149, 150--199, 200--249, 250--349, 350--499 and $\geq 500$~residues. Standard errors were clustered by named genus, retaining genus-unassigned genomes as separate groups. We repeated the analysis using complete interior ORFs: calls marked complete, without internal stops, excluding the first and last two genes on each contig. Within-genome and within-genus length-stratified Mantel--Haenszel estimates supplied additional checks; their gene-level intervals do not account for phylogenetic covariance. Canonical and older taxonomy assignments were analysed separately (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:export_adj_completed}, Supplementary Note~S7). Supplementary Notes S2, S3, S5 and S7 give the thresholds, genome selections and external sequence-reference comparisons across MGnify90, GEM, SPIRE, SMAG and 81 independent desert soil metagenomes.

\subsection*{Structures and genomic neighbourhoods}

HH-suite v3.3.0 \citep{steinegger2019hhsuite} built profiles for all 106,042 dark family representatives and 1,061 control representatives with two HHblits iterations against UniRef30 2023\_02, then searched Pfam-A 35.0 and PDB70 (Fig.~\ref{fig:structural_main}a). InterProScan v5.61-93.0 \citep{jones2014interproscan} annotated the family members, the control representatives and all proteins of the species representatives; domain signatures excluded coiled-coil, disorder and AntiFam matches (Fig.~\ref{fig:structural_main}b). For the all-family structural analysis (\texttt{c17\_struct\_summary}; Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:structall}), ESMFold \citep{lin2023esmfold} modelled the representatives of the recurrent dark ($n=105,867$) and control ($n=105,996$) families between 16 and 1,024 residues. Thermoplasmatota-attributed dark families whose ESMFold model had mean pLDDT below 0.70, or no model ($n=9,644$), were modelled with ColabFold v1.5.5 \citep{mirdita2022colabfold} (AlphaFold2-ptm \citep{jumper2021alphafold}, five models, three recycles, no templates) from multiple-sequence alignments against UniRef30 2302 and ColabFoldDB 202108; the rank-one model was retained. Qualification required a model length of at least 100 residues and mean pLDDT of at least 0.70 on a unit scale. Foldseek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} searched all models against AlphaFold Database v6 and a PDB release downloaded on 2~August 2026, the latter as whole chains and confident-region crops. A model matched PDB at a query-normalised TM-score of at least 0.5 and the AlphaFold Database at $E<10^{-3}$. Foldseek clustering used 80\% coverage of both sequences and $E<10^{-3}$, jointly for dark and control models and separately for the dark models. A separate preliminary structural screen of 17,221 dark families (2,838 qualified models) and its paired whole-chain/confident-region PDB benchmark are documented in Supplementary Note S2 and Supplementary Methods (Suppl. Fig.~\ref{fig:structural}).

Genomic-context comparisons required two flanking genes on each side of a focal family member, mapped to annotations by exact coordinates and strand. Families needed complete windows in at least two species. Same-contig pseudo-focal positions supplied positional expectations, and stratified comparisons accounted for species breadth, host phylum and annotation opportunity (Supplementary Note S2). The targeted chaperone-neighbourhood analysis used exact sequence links between representative and catalogue gene calls, complete non-edge loci and both positional and length-matched backgrounds (Supplementary Note S4). Masked pathway-ranking diagnostics and cross-compartment functional comparisons are described in Supplementary Note S6. For the candidate-family audit (Suppl. Table~\ref{tab:cand_audit_completed}; Supplementary Note S7), candidate member loci were independently verified against the refined species-representative genome FASTAs by exact contig recovery, coordinate bounds, translation under genetic code 11 without internal stops, complete ORF calls (\texttt{partial=00}) and interior contig gene rank (excluding the first two and last two genes of each contig and all genes on contigs with at most four genes, \texttt{edge\_gene\_buffer=2}; 217 of 295 checked member loci verified across 19 evaluated families). The 19-family evaluation covered nine de novo ranked dark candidates (from the top five verified Route~A1 pathway-gap-filling and top five Route~A2 exported-dark screens, accounting for one overlap with prior heat-stress candidates), three prior heat-stress candidates, two sequence-annotated comparators lacking curated positive-control proof and five matched annotation-negative exported controls, keeping representative-own structural models (\texttt{ESMFold} or \texttt{ColabFold\_AF2} verified by exact polymer-sequence match and gated at $\geq 100$~aa, mean $\mathrm{pLDDT}\geq 0.70$ and query-normalised TM-score $\geq 0.50$) separate from non-representative member structures and profiles, building MAFFT v7.525 alignments and IQ-TREE 3 (\texttt{LG+G4}) trees for families with at least four non-redundant verified sequences, and evaluating specific neighbourhood guild adjacency by 1,000 circular gene-order shifts across contigs with at least five genes. Extended context screening of 1,460 families across six role hypotheses, the 47-family follow-up audit, metabolic gap-filling sensitivity under altered biomass templates and masked-gene recovery, sequence-qualified host--virus candidate matching, and independent proteogenomic search eligibility controls are described in Supplementary Note S8.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis and reproducibility}

Statistical methods followed the sampling unit and endpoint. Paired site contrasts used Wilcoxon tests; reference hit-rate comparisons used paired query outcomes; within-lineage associations used phylogenetic generalised least squares. Family-context comparisons used stratified permutations. Bootstrap intervals resampled sites or families as specified for each endpoint. Holm or Benjamini--Hochberg adjustments applied within declared test blocks. The ecological and figure analyses used NumPy v2.2.6, pandas v2.3.3 and SciPy v1.15.3; versions and seeds for the other analyses are retained with their scripts. Supplementary Methods specify model covariates, permutation constraints, diagnostic analyses and cohort exclusions. Machine-readable input manifests link reported results to retained source files.
