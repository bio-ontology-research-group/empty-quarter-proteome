## One-line significance (the hook)
Earth's largest, most arid sand sea is built from **taxonomically ordinary microbes carrying extraordinary
biology** — an integrated survival proteome we can read, wrapped around a vast, real, environmentally-organised
protein dark matter we cannot — and the reference databases that "recognise" these organisms predict neither.

## The narrative engine — two expectation-subversions
1. **The tree is boring.** An extreme, isolated biome *should* hold novel deep lineages; GTDB says it does not
   (conventional arid-soil profile, 0 novel phyla). ⇒ Novelty is not phylogenetic but in **gene content, fold
   space and metabolism**. [@parks2018gtdb; @chaumeil2022gtdbtk]
2. **The databases lie by omission.** References *classify* these taxa yet cannot decode most of their proteins
   [@pavlopoulos2023darkmatter], predict their gene content from 16S [@douglas2020picrust2], or anticipate
   their folds [@durairaj2023folds]. ⇒ "Known" at the name level ≠ known at the biology level.

## Central thesis — the spine (read this to understand why the parts cohere)
The desert is the **instrument, not the subject**. The deliverable is a clean, gradient-resolved demonstration
that microbial ecology's foundational inference — **taxonomy ⇒ biology** — fails systematically, and a map of
*where the missing biology actually lives* (endemic genes, novel folds, community-distributed metabolism).
Parts A, B and C are **one organism-set viewed three ways**, each widening the gap between *classified* and
*understood*:
- **A** proves we can read *some* of the biology (a coherent extreme-survival proteome) — establishing these
  are real, adapted organisms, not artefacts.
- **B** proves we *cannot* read *most* of it (a real, selection-maintained, endemic, structurally-novel dark
  proteome) — the unknown is biology, not backlog.
- **C** proves that even what we nominally "know" is **not predictable or readable per-genome** (16S→function
  fails; function is distributed across the community) — and that none of this is contamination/undersampling.
The hyperarid, low-anthropogenic, never-sampled setting is what makes the test *clean* (minimal kit-ome
confound; strong, measurable environmental gradients; a taxonomically "known" community as the control case).

## 1. Introduction
**1.1 The unknown is the rule.** Most microbial taxa remain uncultured and most of the protein universe is
uncharacterised ("functional/structural dark matter"); function assignment has plateaued against a hard,
irreducible unknown core. [@steen2019uncultured; @pavlopoulos2023darkmatter; @vanni2022agnostos; @durairaj2023folds;
@barriohernandez2023clustering; @rodriguezdelrio2024fesnov; @li2025fugassem]
**1.2 Biology or backlog?** Global catalogues lack the fine environmental gradients needed to test whether the
unknown is real and organised vs an annotation artefact. [@richardson2023mgnify; @duan2024gmsc]
**1.3 Deserts: extreme, energy-limited, undersampled — expected to be novel, and globally important.** Drylands
are the largest terrestrial biome and expanding under climate change [@huang2016dryland]; aridity is a master
variable that reduces microbial diversity and decouples nutrient cycles [@maestre2015aridity;
@delgadobaquerizo2013decoupling]. Hyperarid edaphic systems are ruled by oligotrophy, desiccation, UV and
osmotic stress, with large dormant "seed banks" and rare-biosphere reservoirs; the classic expectation is
exotic, deep lineages, and prior desert work emphasises "actinobacterial dark matter". [@makhalanyane2015microbial;
@fierer2017soil; @lebre2017xerotolerant; @lennon2011seedbanks; @lynch2015rarebiosphere; @sogin2006rarebiosphere;
@bull2016atacama; @idris2017atacama]
**1.4 System.** Rub' al-Khali = largest contiguous sand sea, among Earth's most arid, never genome-sampled at
this scale; dense sampling with XRF geochemistry (incl. V), climate, depth/compartment. Local prior microbiology
is sparse and amplicon/PICRUSt-level. [@edgell2006arabian; @alotaibi2020sabkha; @khan2020aridsoil]
**1.5 This study.** Characterise the *whole proteome* at three levels — functions we can read (A), the dark
proteome we cannot (B), and the bridge explaining why "known" organisms still defeat references (C). Frame:
**known organisms, unknown biology.**

### PART B — THE PROTEOME WE DON'T: a real, structured, endemic dark proteome

**B1 — Scale: sequence *and* structural dark matter.** A single, explicitly-defined novelty ladder (so the
claim is unambiguous and not a count-race vs global fold surveys): 51.9M proteins → mmseqs2 reps → **2.84M

reps; no AFDB hit)** → clustered into **334,810 novel multi-member families** → **68,808 strict novel-fold
families** (`is_novel_family`: ≥2 members, zero AFDB hit). Argument: dark matter here is not just unannotated
sequence but **unseen folds**, at family scale. Every fold-count claim is paired with the **validation in B1a**
(pLDDT, family recurrence across MAGs, explicit-structure FoldSeek reconfirmation) — the novelty is anchored on
*ecology + validation*, never on count alone (cf. AFESM/Durairaj global surveys). [@pavlopoulos2023darkmatter;
@lin2023esmfold; @durairaj2023folds; @barriohernandez2023clustering; @vankempen2024foldseek; @heinzinger2024prostt5;
@varadi2022alphafolddb; @jumper2021alphafold; @steinegger2017mmseqs2] [DONE] [RESULT]

  subset mean 0.78) and per-family recurrence (n MAGs/family) → report raw and high-confidence-filtered counts.

### PART C — THE CONCEPTUAL PAYLOAD: why "known" organisms defeat references (Subversion 2)
*(co-equal pillar, not an appendix: the reference-failure proof + its open-pangenome mechanism + the endemism
controls are the least-scoopable, most general contribution — the part that reframes microbial inference broadly.)*

**C1 — Reference bias: 16S "knows" the taxa but cannot predict their genes.** PICRUSt2-from-amplicon vs
MAG-encoded reality across **436 samples with both 16S predictions and genome-resolved MAGs** (KO content;
CoverM-weighted, NSTI-screened). The result is *not* a placement failure — weighted NSTI is typical-soil
(median 0.16, well under the <2 cutoff), so ASVs place fine — but gene content fails three ways: **(1) it
overpredicts a generic reference metabolism** (median 10,543 KOs predicted/sample vs 4,482 actually encoded,
2.4×; 6,363 false-positive KOs/sample; e.g. `hoxH` predicted in 99% of samples but encoded in 0.2%, Fe-only
nitrogenase `anfG` 99% vs 0%); **(2) abundances of the very signature functions are decoupled** — for
atmospheric-CO `coxLMS`, osmolyte `otsAB`/`ect`, UV-repair `phr`, predicted-vs-encoded abundance Spearman ≈ 0
to negative (photolyase −0.12); **(3) the dominant failure — a hard KO-space ceiling**: 56.7% of all MAG
proteins carry *no* KO (19.1% no eggNOG ortholog at all), so the majority of the proteome — including, by
construction, all the structural dark matter (Part B) — is invisible to any 16S-based predictor (PICRUSt2
recovers the housekeeping core, median 93% presence-recall, but nothing beyond it). Argument: classification ≠
functional predictability; the methodological pillar reinforces the dark-matter pillar. [@douglas2020picrust2;
@langille2013picrust; @sun2020inference; @matchado2024limits; @tettelin2005pangenome; @khan2020aridsoil]
[DONE — 436 matched samples, 15,469 MAGs] [RESULT in results/picrust2_bias/SUMMARY.md]

**C1a — *Why* it fails: open pangenomes make gene content unpredictable from taxonomy (mechanism, from first
principles).** Cluster MAGs into species (dRep ANI≥95) and build per-species pangenome-saturation curves:
**core/housekeeping genes are closed** (saturate early → predictable from a name, which is why PICRUSt2 recovers
the housekeeping core) while the **ecologically-decisive genes — osmolytes, trace-gas metabolism, BGCs, and the
dark families — are open** (never saturate → unpredictable). Then the **B↔C seam:** the open-pangenome accessory
fraction and the dark proteome substantially overlap, so reference bias is a *diversity* problem, not merely a
*depth* problem — the databases lack genes that are inherently variable *within* a classified species. Argument:
elevates C1 from empirical observation to a predicted-from-theory result; unifies Parts B and C. [@tettelin2005pangenome;
@mcinerney2017openpangenome; @rodriguezdelrio2024fesnov] [PLANNED — per-species ortholog clustering] [RESULT pending]

**C2 — Community function: pervasive, broadly-distributed cross-feeding (but not guild-anchored).** Genome-
scale models (gapseq) on per-sample communities + SMETANA global MRO + an LP-based FBA cross-feeding-potential
analysis (commercial MILP solvers were access-restricted on the cluster, so SMETANA *detailed* was intractable —
documented). Findings: (i) **moderate-high resource overlap** (MRO ≈ 0.7) — a competition signal typical of
oligotrophic resource sharing; (ii) **pervasive organic cross-feeding potential** across all taxa; (iii) the
atmospheric "life-on-air" guild are **NOT preferential metabolite donors** (guild ≈ non-guild donor potential —
an honest negative), robust to a completeness control (ρ≈0). Argument: function is *distributed across the whole
community*, not concentrated in one guild and not readable per-genome — which still defeats single-genome
reference logic, just not via the guild-as-feeder route. [@zelezniak2015smetana; @machado2021polarization;
@zimmermann2021gapseq; @morris2012blackqueen; @zengler2018social; @dsouza2018crossfeeding] [DONE — 16-sample pilot]
[RESULT in results/smetana/CROSSFEED.md]
- *Caveat (load-bearing):* GEMs see only the **decodable** metabolism; the guild's distinctive contribution may
  live in the dark proteome (invisible here). Complete-medium FBA = potential upper bound, not realised flux.

**C3 — It is not an artefact: contamination, global, abundance controls.** Kit-ome/contamination ruled out
(pathogen census; low-biomass best practice); read-recruitment (CoverM) shows novel fractions are abundant, not
rare. **EQ-specificity vs undersampling** is benchmarked by searching the 23,382 dark-family reps against global
catalogues under one common criterion (DIAMOND `--sensitive` e<1e-3): **(i)** vs **MGnify90** (718M env protein
clusters) — **36.6% are absent entirely** (endemic), and of the 63.4% with any global homolog only **9.7% are
ever seen in terrestrial/soil samples** (most surface in aquatic 83.5% / engineered biomes) — i.e. almost none
of the EQ *soil* dark proteome is represented in *soil* globally; **(ii)** vs **GEM** (52k MAGs, 123M proteins)
— **47.4% absent**, i.e. nearly half the dark families have no homolog even in the most comprehensive
genome-resolved catalogue of Earth's microbiomes; **(iii)** vs **five other deserts** (Atacama, Mojave, arid,
trace-gas-autotrophy desert, Antarctic cold-desert) — **42–89% absent** per desert, and **31.9% absent from the
union of all five deserts** (147M proteins), so EQ dark matter is *not* generic arid-soil dark matter shared
across deserts (+ SPIRE rep. genomes [finishing]). Crucially, **endemism does not vanish as reference depth

## 3. Discussion
- **Reframing novelty.** Discovery need not be phylogenetic; the richest unknown sits inside ordinary taxa, in
  function/structure/metabolism. [@fierer2017soil; @rodriguezdelrio2024fesnov]
- **Reference-database failure as a general warning.** If 16S→function and per-genome annotation fail in a
  *classified* biome, the blind spot affects soil-microbiome inference broadly. [@matchado2024limits;
  @khan2020aridsoil]
- **The desert as a discovery reservoir.** Endemic dark families, novel folds, diversifying recognition
  proteins, and an atmospheric/osmolyte survival toolkit = targets for enzyme/fold discovery + an arid/astrobiology
  analogue. [@durairaj2023folds; @schulzemakuch2018transitory]

## 6. Honest negatives (kept explicit)
Conventional taxonomy (no novel phyla); structured ≠ adaptive (no aridity tracking; category-level decode↔
ecology null); vanadium null (reproduced at single-family resolution); osmolyte not enriched as a *class*;
trace-gas enzymes NOT supplied by dark matter; VFDB "virulence" dual-use, collapsing at high identity; As/Hg–XRF
link non-cognate; effect sizes modest; **atmospheric guild are NOT the metabolic donors (guild-as-feeder
refuted; cross-feeding is broadly distributed, MRO-competition-leaning)**; community cross-feeding bounded by
GEM decodability + complete-medium upper bound + no commercial MILP solver (SMETANA detailed deferred);
**PICRUSt2 recovers the housekeeping core (median 93% presence-recall, NSTI typical-soil) — the reference-bias
claim is the *quantitative/dark-majority* failure, not a placement or core-recovery failure (stated honestly).**
These bound the claims and set the wet-lab agenda.

## 7. Competing hypotheses & falsifiability (what would break the story)
Each pillar is framed as a null the data must defeat — this is the reviewer-facing rigor spine:
- **"Dark matter is spurious ORFs / annotation backlog."** → defeated by pervasive purifying selection (B2);
  would resurface if dark families showed dN/dS ≈ 1 or no selection signal. [@nei1986dnds; @rodriguezdelrio2024fesnov]
- **"Novelty is undersampling, not desert-specificity."** → tested by global-catalogue comparison (C3); the
  story weakens if EQ "endemic" families turn out merely rare-but-present elsewhere. [@nayfach2021gem; @schmidt2024spire]
- **"The pristine/all-novel signal is contamination/kit-ome."** → tested by pathogen + low-biomass controls
  (A5/C3); a contamination bloom (named pathogens, reagent taxa) would falsify "clean biome." [@salter2014contamination; @davis2018decontam]
- **"Endemism is just dispersal limitation."** → addressed by the Baas Becking framing + aeolian-transport
  context (B3); environment-tracking that survives spatial control argues selection over pure dispersal. [@dewit2006everything; @kellogg2006aerobiology]
- **"Cross-feeding is a MAG-incompleteness/gap-fill artefact."** → tested by the completeness-sensitivity
  control (C2); a strong receiver∼completeness correlation would mark it artefactual. [@machado2021polarization]
- **"16S fails only because of undersampling / wrong taxonomy, not a real property of the organisms."** →
  tested by the open-pangenome mechanism (C1a): if even *within* a GTDB-defined species the ecologically-
  decisive genes saturate (closed pangenome), the reference-failure thesis weakens; if they stay open while
  housekeeping closes, prediction-from-taxonomy is impossible in principle. [@tettelin2005pangenome]
- **THE load-bearing risk — "reference bias is overstated."** → make-or-break test now run (C1, 436 matched
  samples): PICRUSt2 recovers the housekeeping core but overpredicts a generic reference metabolism (2.4× too many
  KOs), mis-ranks the signature functions' abundances, and is structurally blind to 56.7% of the proteome — the
  thesis holds on the quantitative/dark-majority axis, not on a (disproven) placement-failure axis. The honest
  framing — "it gets the easy core right and everything that matters wrong" — is itself reviewer-armour.
  [@douglas2020picrust2; @sun2020inference]
